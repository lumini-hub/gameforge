Chapter 2: The Bet — Anton ChekhovSection A · asked in 2024 (Q1 and Q2) · Russian short story (1889)

2.1 Must-know summary

ElementDetails
Author & genreAnton Chekhov (Russian writer, 1860–1904). A short story with a moral and psychological focus.
SettingA dark, windy autumn night in the banker's house; the story looks back fifteen years to a party in the same house. The lawyer is confined in a lodge in the banker's garden.
NarrationThird person. Begins in the present (the last night) and then goes back in a flashback to the party fifteen years earlier.
Main charactersThe banker — rich, proud, spoilt and frivolous; later ruined and desperate. The young lawyer — 25 at the start, intelligent, idealistic, determined; changes into a wise man who despises wealth. Minor: guests, the watchman, servant.
The betAt a party the guests argue about capital punishment vs life imprisonment. The banker says death is more humane; the lawyer says "to live in any way is better than not at all". The banker bets two million that the lawyer cannot bear solitary confinement for fifteen years.
RulesThe lawyer stays alone in the lodge, may not see or speak to anyone, may send notes and ask for books, music, wine. If he leaves even a few minutes early, the banker is free from paying.
Plot in briefFirst years: the lawyer suffers, reads light novels, plays the piano. Later he studies languages, philosophy and history; then the Gospels, theology, science and poetry. Meanwhile the banker loses his fortune by gambling and speculation. On the last night he plans to kill the lawyer to avoid paying, finds him asleep and reads his letter: the lawyer despises money and all worldly things and will leave five minutes early to forfeit the prize. The banker kisses him and weeps, feeling great contempt for himself. Next morning the watchman reports the lawyer has fled; the banker locks the letter in his safe.
Themesgreed and the corrupting power of money • true value of knowledge • moral transformation • capital punishment / value of life • freedom vs imprisonment (the banker is the real prisoner of money) • pride and shame.
Literary devicesFlashback (opening), irony (the "prisoner" wins; the rich man loses), contrast / foil (banker vs lawyer), symbolism (the lodge = isolation, books = knowledge, the safe = greed), suspense (the night, the planned murder), foreshadowing (the banker's growing money worries).
MoralMaterial wealth is worthless compared with wisdom, self-control and freedom of mind.

2024 A-Q1(b)(i)People, in general, are after money. Then what made the lawyer renounce his bet of two millions at the end? Explain with TWO reasons. [5]

The lawyer renounced the two million because fifteen years of reading changed his values. Firstly, his study of books, philosophy and religion taught him that wealth, pleasure and even human wisdom are temporary and empty, so money lost its attraction. Secondly, he wanted to show his complete contempt for the banker's greed, so he left five minutes before the deadline and deliberately forfeited the prize.

≈ 65 words
2024 A-Q1(b)(ii)Who do you think is the eventual winner in The Bet? Justify with TWO reasons. [5]

In my opinion, the lawyer is the eventual winner. Firstly, he survives fifteen years of solitude and emerges wiser and free from greed and pride, which is a greater victory than money. Secondly, he voluntarily gives up the two million, showing great moral strength, whereas the banker, ruined and ashamed after planning a murder, loses his dignity.

≈ 57 words
2024 A-Q2(a)Contrast the banker in The Bet and the White Knight in The White Knight by outlining the events in their respective lives that change them. [10]

The banker in The Bet and the White Knight both begin with false confidence, but different events change them.

At the start the banker is rich, proud and frivolous, and he makes the bet carelessly. Over fifteen years he gambles and speculates and loses his fortune, so on the last night he plans to kill the lawyer to avoid paying. After reading the lawyer's letter of contempt for money, however, he is ashamed, kisses the sleeping lawyer and weeps.

The White Knight begins as a self-righteous man who sees himself as the symbol of good and rides out to destroy the Black Knight. Always arriving too late, he grows hungry and desperate, steals buns and a horse, and finally kills an innocent youth. Seeing his armour turned black, he realises that he has become the evil he hunted.

Thus the banker is changed by shame, while the knight is changed by shock.

≈ 152 words
2024 A-Q2(b)The lawyer undergoes a transformation by reading different books. Name a book you have read and explain how it changed your thoughts and actions. [10]

The lawyer in The Bet changes from a young man who wanted to win an argument into a wise man who despises wealth, because books opened his mind. A book that changed me is Wings of Fire, the autobiography of A. P. J. Abdul Kalam.

Before reading it, I gave up quickly whenever a task was difficult, and I believed that success belonged only to the rich or the lucky. Kalam grew up in a modest family, sold newspapers as a boy and missed his dream of becoming a fighter pilot, yet he never stopped working and became a great scientist and President. His life taught me that perseverance matters more than circumstances.

After reading the book, I set clear goals, made a daily study timetable and began to treat failures as lessons. I also started helping my juniors with their studies, as Kalam believed in sharing knowledge. Like the lawyer, I learned that a good book can change a person.

Chapter 3: The White Knight — Eric NicolSection A · asked in 2025 (Q1, Q2) and 2024 (Q2a) · a modern fairy-tale allegory

3.1 Must-know summary

ElementDetails
Author & genreEric Nicol (Canadian humorist and writer). A short allegory / satirical fairy tale (it opens "Once upon a time there was a knight…").
SettingA little castle at the edge of the forest of Life; villages, an inn, a baker's shop and the forest where the knight travels — a symbolic world.
CharactersThe White Knight — proud, self-righteous, sure that he stands for good. The Black Knight — the evil he hunts; never seen. The young knight in shining white armour — the innocent youth. Villagers: baker, innkeeper and daughter.
Plot(1) The knight looks into a mirror, sees a White Knight and decides to find and kill the Black Knight (evil) in single combat. (2) Rides off but is always too late: the baker's shop robbed, a horse stolen, the innkeeper's daughter wronged. (3) Tired, hungry and short of money, he steals buns, swaps his lame horse for another at night; his armour is stained by the long quest. (4) In the forest a youth in gleaming white armour charges him; the knight kills him. (5) The dying youth asks whether evil has won; the knight sees his own darkened armour black beside the youth's brightness and realises he has become the Black Knight.
Themesappearance vs reality • self-deception • self-righteousness is dangerous • good and evil exist within one person • heroism is judged by actions, not by looks or intentions • the need for self-awareness.
Symbolsmirror = self-image/vanity • white armour = claimed goodness • black armour = hidden guilt • Black Knight = evil/the shadow in ourselves • forest of Life = life's journey • the quest = the search for evil outside while ignoring one's own faults.
DevicesIrony (he hunts evil and becomes it), allegory, symbolism, satire, fairy-tale opening, third-person narration.
MoralDo not trust appearances or your own self-image; check your actions. People who are sure they are right can do great harm.
Check with your textbookThe summary above follows standard study guides of the story. Your textbook version may use slightly different details (the order of crimes, the closing lines). Always quote or refer to events from your copy.

Chapter 4: The Imp and the Crust — Leo TolstoySection A · asked in 2025 (Q1 and Q2) · a moral folk tale (1886)

4.1 Must-know summary

ElementDetails
Author & genreLeo Tolstoy (Russian novelist, 1828–1910). A moral folk tale / fable with devils and a simple peasant.
SettingA Russian village — the peasant's field, then his farm and house.
CharactersThe poor peasant — hard-working, kind, forgiving. The imp — a little devil sent to tempt him. The Devil (chief devil) — the imp's master. Neighbours and a poor man at the feast.
Plot(1) A poor peasant goes to plough, wraps his bread in his coat and hides it under a bush. The imp steals the bread and waits behind the bush to hear him curse. (2) The peasant, hungry, says that whoever took it must have needed it — "may it do him good"; he feels no anger, so the imp fails. (3) The Devil threatens the imp that if he cannot win the peasant in three years, he will be ducked in holy water. (4) The imp comes back as a hired labourer and gives clever advice that brings the peasant a huge harvest, even in a bad year. (5) With the surplus grain the imp teaches him to make vodka. (6) The peasant grows rich, proud and greedy, and drinks. He becomes miserly, bullying and finally brutal. (7) At a drunken party he refuses a poor man a drink ("I can't find drink for everyone who comes along"). (8) The imp reports his success; the Devil praises and rewards him.
TitleThe crust stands for the peasant's early simple life and generosity — he could forgive the theft of his last crust and think of the thief's need; it is also the first test the imp fails. By the end the peasant refuses even a drink to the poor.
Themeswealth and greed corrupt • temptation • kindness and forgiveness vs anger • alcohol and vice destroy character • simplicity and sharing are true goodness.
DevicesDramatic irony (the reader knows the labourer is the imp), symbolism (crust = simple life/sharing; vodka = vice; imp = temptation), personification of evil, contrast (the peasant before and after wealth), a fable's moral.
MoralA man is tested not by poverty but by wealth. Easy riches and bad habits can destroy goodness more surely than hardship.
Check with your textbookDetails of the middle of the story (the exact advice the imp gives and the order of events at the party) can differ between translations. Use the events above and add small details from your own copy.

2025 A-Q2(a)Both the poor peasant in The Imp and the Crust and the knight in The White Knight are initially presented as having positive qualities. Using details from the stories, explain how their characters get transformed. [10]

Both the peasant and the knight begin as admirable men, but each is transformed for the worse.

In The Imp and the Crust the peasant is hard-working, kind and forgiving. When the imp steals his bread, he says that the thief must have needed it. After the imp's clever advice brings him a huge harvest, he makes vodka from the surplus grain. Wealth and drink make him proud, greedy and cruel, and at the party he refuses even a drink to a poor man.

The White Knight begins as a brave, idealistic man who believes that he stands for good and sets out to kill the Black Knight. Failure and hunger drive him to steal buns and a horse, and finally he kills an innocent youth. His blackened armour shows that he has become the evil he hunted.

Thus the peasant is corrupted by temptation and wealth, while the knight is corrupted by his own self-righteousness.

2025 A-Q2(b)Imagine you are the imp and are frustrated by your initial failure with the peasant. Considering the peasant's character and the innate human weaknesses, explain TWO alternative strategies (other than those in the text) to tempt him into sin. [10]

I am furious that the peasant forgave me, but I know that every human has a weakness. Since he is kind and trusting, I shall use two new strategies.

First, I shall use pride. I shall make the villagers praise him as the wisest and most generous man in the area. Humans love flattery, so he will soon begin to think that he is better than his neighbours. His pride will make him look down on the poor, and a proud man cannot easily forgive or help others.

Second, I shall use jealousy. I shall arrange for his neighbour to buy a finer ox and build a bigger house. When the peasant compares himself with the neighbour, he will begin to envy him. Envy will turn into anger, then into quarrels and revenge.

Once he is proud and jealous, the peasant's gentle heart will be lost and I shall be rewarded.

Chapter 5: Essay — The Titanic: History of a DisasterSection B · asked in 2024 · expository essay, chronological narration

5.1 Must-know summary of the passage

AspectDetails from the passage
Genre & purposeInformative / expository essay (adapted from the NOAA website). It explains what happened and why it matters.
Text type & orderChronological narration — events in time order (warnings → collision → lifeboats → panic → sinking → rescue → impact).
Thesis (main idea)The Titanic disaster was largely a result of human error and arrogance — ignored or unclear warnings and too few lifeboats — and it led to major changes in maritime safety.
Key factsBritish luxury liner, maiden voyage, sank on 14 April 1912 with 2240 passengers and crew. Many iceberg warnings; unclear if they reached the control room. Designed for about 3500 people but had lifeboats for about 1200. Captain: Edward John Smith.
EventsAbout 12:30 a.m.: only women and children to the lifeboats • 1:30 a.m.: panic; on the port side passengers threatened to jump into a lifeboat meant for 65 but holding 28; an officer fired three shots to scare them • the last lifeboat had 47 spaces: crew formed a circle and only women and children passed • more than 1500 still on board when the stern lifted • 2:20 a.m.: the ship stood upright, then sank • RMS Carpathia arrived about two hours later and rescued 700+.
Expert viewWalter Lord (The Night Lives On): if First Officer Murdoch had steamed right at the iceberg instead of trying to avoid it, he might have saved the ship.
ImpactNew maritime laws: enough lifeboats, improved safety procedures, an international ice patrol. The tragedy became a symbol of human hubris (excessive pride) and the cost of ignoring safety.
Human dramasMen saw off wives and children; families separated; selfless people gave up their seats for the vulnerable.

2024 B-Q1(b)(i)The story of the Titanic is often seen as a cautionary tale about human arrogance and the consequences of disregarding safety measures. Explain briefly. [5]

The Titanic is a warning against arrogance. The crew received several iceberg warnings, yet the ship did not slow down or change course, as if nothing could harm it. The ship also carried lifeboats for only about 1200 of the 3500 people it could hold. This careless attitude cost more than 1500 lives and later forced the world to make stricter safety laws.

2024 B-Q1(b)(ii)Give your opinion on why the sinking of the Titanic still continues to captivate people's interest and imagination even after a century. [5]

In my opinion, the Titanic still fascinates people for two reasons. First, it combines luxury, ambition and a preventable tragedy: the greatest ship of its time sank on its first voyage. Second, it contains powerful human stories, such as husbands saying goodbye to families and people giving up their seats, which touch our emotions. The unanswered question of whether the warnings reached the control room adds mystery.

2024 B-Q2(a)The text uses chronological narration. Rewrite the essay in a paragraph using cause and effect narration. [10]

Method: start from the cause, link with because, as a result, therefore, consequently, this led to, end with the effects.

The Titanic sank mainly because warnings were ignored and safety was neglected. Several ships sent iceberg warnings, but it is uncertain whether they reached the control room, and the ship did not change its course in time. As a result, the iceberg tore large holes in the hull, and water flooded several compartments, so the ship began to sink rapidly. Because the Titanic carried lifeboats for only about 1200 of the 3500 people it could hold, there were not enough places for everyone. Consequently, the captain ordered women and children to be loaded first, and panic broke out among the passengers; an officer even fired three shots to control the crowd. Because there were too few lifeboats, and some left half empty, more than 1500 people were still on board when the ship stood upright at 2:20 a.m. and went down. This tragedy led to new laws that require enough lifeboats, better safety procedures and an international ice patrol.

2024 B-Q2(b)You are Wilson, a reporter from BBC, who is among the survivors of the Titanic tragedy. Write a report on the sinking of Titanic. [10]

Titanic Sinks on Her Maiden Voyage

By Wilson, BBC (survivor and eyewitness)

On board RMS Carpathia, 15 April 1912: The luxury liner Titanic sank in the north Atlantic after striking an iceberg on her maiden voyage, and more than 1500 people were lost. I was among about 700 survivors who were rescued.

The iceberg tore large holes in the ship's hull and water flooded several compartments. Although many warnings had been received that night, the ship did not avoid the ice. Around 12:30 a.m. the officers ordered women and children into the lifeboats. There were boats for only about 1200 of the 3500 people the ship could carry. By 1:30 a.m. there was panic, and an officer fired three shots to calm the crowd. At 2:20 a.m. the ship stood upright and disappeared. The Carpathia reached us about two hours later.

Many men stayed behind so that their wives and children could escape. Officials say that stricter safety rules must now follow.

Chapter 6: Essay — A Brief History of ChocolateSection B · asked in 2025 · expository essay, adapted from the Smithsonian Magazine

6.1 Must-know summary of the passage

AspectDetails from the passage
Genre & purposeExpository (informative) essay: it explains the history, meaning and economic importance of chocolate. (It is neither narrative nor argumentative.)
Main ideaChocolate is "the best-known food that nobody knows anything about" (Alexandra Leaf): for about 90% of its history it was a bitter drink, not a sweet bar, and it has been valued as currency and a global industry.
Termscacao = the plant/beans before processing • chocolate = anything made from the beans • cocoa = the powdered form. Origin of the word: Aztec xocoatl (bitter drink). Latin name Theobroma cacao = "food of the gods".
Early historyAround for about 2000 years (maybe longer). In pre-modern Latin America beans were currency: 1 bean = a tamale; 100 beans = a good turkey hen.
EuropeMontezuma welcomed Cortes with a banquet, mistaking him for a returning deity. Spaniards first disliked the drink ("a bitter drink for pigs") but, mixed with honey or cane sugar, it became popular in Spain; by the 17th century a fashionable drink for the rich (Casanova liked it).
Industrial ageThe steam engine (late 1700s) made mass production possible. 1828: a Dutch chemist made powdered "Dutch cocoa". 1847: Joseph Fry made the first modern chocolate bar. 1868: Cadbury sold boxed chocolates. Later milk chocolate by Nestlé.
AmericaChocolate was in soldiers' rations (and used in lieu of wages) in the Revolutionary War. Today a more than $4 billion industry in the US; the average American eats about half a pound a month.
Useful vocabularyMeaning

etymologistsexperts who study the origin of words
reincarnated deitya god come back to life
aphrodisiacsomething believed to increase love/desire
humblemodest, ordinary

Poet & formDouglas Malloch (American poet, 1877–1938), the "lumberman's poet". Four stanzas of six lines each, rhymed in couplets: AABBCC in every stanza (fight/light, plain/rain, king/thing).
Central ideaStruggle makes strength. A tree (and a person) that grows without hardship stays small and ordinary; one that faces storms becomes strong, tall and wise.
Stanza 1A tree that never had to fight for light and air, standing safe in an open plain with enough rain, never becomes a "forest king" — it stays a stunted "scrubby thing".

2025 C-Q2(a)The poem "To My Mother" is a sonnet. List any FOUR features of sonnet used in the poem and explain. [10]

A sonnet is a fourteen-line poem about a single idea. "To My Mother" shows four features of the form.

First, it has fourteen lines, printed as eight lines followed by six. Second, it is divided into an octave and a sestet. The octave (lines 1–8) paints the portrait of the mother, while the sestet (lines 9–14) deals with her courage and the son's faith. Third, there is a turn (volta) at line 9, "She will not glance up at the bomber", where the poem moves from a happy picture to her courage during war. Fourth, it follows a fixed rhyme pattern, ABCDABCD EFGEFG, using slant rhymes such as far/for, laughter/after and mountain/morning.

Finally, like a traditional sonnet, the poem develops one idea, love and admiration for the mother, and closes with a resolution: she will "move from mourning into morning".

2025 D-Q2(b)How is the use of precise language shown to instill control and obedience in the community? Support your points using evidence from the text. [10]

In Jonas's community, language is carefully controlled so that people cannot think or feel beyond the rules.

First, children are corrected whenever their words are not exact. Jonas is told that "frightened" is the wrong word and he should say "apprehensive", and he is told to avoid exaggerated words like "starving". This trains people to hide their feelings and to accept the system.

Second, harsh realities are hidden behind polite words. "Release" means killing, and "Elsewhere" hides the truth about where people go. Because the words sound gentle, nobody questions what happens.

Third, rules and announcements are communicated by a speaker, and everyone must reply politely with set phrases, such as apologising and accepting apologies. This produces habit and obedience rather than thought.

Thus precise language limits thought, emotion and rebellion.
