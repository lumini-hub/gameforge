  • Absent in a prokaryote: mitochondria P25 Q1a(i): C. Unique to prokaryotes: the nucleoid (and plasmid) P25 Q3b. A ciliated organism with a visible nucleus is eukaryotic P23 Q1a(i): B.
  • Why prokaryotes matter: decomposers (recycle nutrients), nitrogen fixers, gut bacteria (digestion, vitamin K and B), curd and fermentation, bioremediation, biogas.

The cell membrane & types of transport

  • The membrane is a phospholipid bilayer with proteins (fluid mosaic model); it is selectively (semi-) permeable: it lets some substances pass and blocks others.
  • Diffusion: movement of molecules from high to low concentration (down the gradient); no energy. E.g. smell of ema datsi spreading in a room P24 Q1a(ii): D; O2 and CO2 exchange.
  • Facilitated diffusion: diffusion of larger or charged molecules (glucose, ions) through carrier / channel proteins; down the gradient; no ATP P25 Q1d(ii): a.
  • Osmosis: movement of water from a region of low solute (high water) concentration to high solute concentration through a semipermeable membrane P25 Q1a(ii): D.
  • Active transport: movement against the gradient (low → high) using carrier proteins and energy (ATP): e.g. mineral ions into root hairs, glucose uptake, Na+–K+ pump.

  • Salting fish, olives or vegetables: the hypertonic brine draws water out of bacteria, which die of dehydration P24 Q1b(i): hypertonic.
  • Wilting in salt water: the soil water becomes hypertonic, so roots lose water by osmosis and leaves lose turgor P25 Q6a.
  • Soaking seeds: water enters the seed by osmosis/imbibition; the seed coat softens and the embryo activates, so germination is faster P23 Q6a.
  • Wax on fruits reduces water loss (transpiration) and membrane transport, so fruits stay fresh longer: statements I and III → D P23 Q1a(iv).

Experiments you must know

  • Potato osmoscope: a cavity is scooped in a potato and filled with sugar (or salt) solution; a pin marks the initial level; the potato stands in a beaker of water. After about 1 hour the level in the cavity rises because water moves by osmosis from the water (high water concentration) through the potato tissue (semipermeable) into the concentrated solution.
  • Control: a boiled (dead) potato, or a cavity with water instead of sugar. Keep the same potato size, volume of liquid, temperature and time.
  • Potato cubes in sugar solutions: cube gains volume in dilute solutions (hypotonic), no change in the isotonic one, and shrinks in strong solutions (hypertonic).

2 · Photosynthesis
P25 Q1a(iii,xvi,xxiv),c(i),Q2e,Q5a,Q7a,Q8a · P24 Q1a(iii,iv),c(ii),Q2b,Q8c · P23 Q1a(v,vi),b(ii),c(i),Q3b,Q4d,Q6e

Key ideas

  • Equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O →(light, chlorophyll)→ C6H12O6 + 6O2. Plants make glucose from CO2 and water using light; photosynthesis removes CO2 and so helps to minimise global warming P23 Q1b(ii).
  • Chlorophyll (in the chloroplast) absorbs light energy (mainly red and blue, reflects green) and changes it to chemical energy P25 Q2e.
  • Chloroplast: double membrane; grana = stacks of thylakoids (hold chlorophyll) → light reaction; the fluid stroma → dark reaction (Calvin cycle) P24 Q2b(i).

  • Order of events in the light reaction: II splitting of water and IV formation of ATP → C P25 Q1a(xvi). Products passed to the Calvin cycle: NADPH and ATP → D P23 Q1a(v). Role of NADPH in the dark reaction: reduces CO2 to glucose → A P25 Q1a(xxiv).
  • Dark reaction can occur in day and night as it is light-independent → TRUE P24 Q1c(ii) (check).

Factors affecting the rate of photosynthesis

  • External: light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature, water. Internal: chlorophyll content, leaf structure. So chlorophyll content is NOT external → B P24 Q1a(iv) (check).
  • Light intensity / CO2: rate rises then levels off (another factor becomes limiting). So “higher light always gives a higher rate regardless of other factors” is FALSE P25 Q1c(i).
  • Temperature: rate rises to an optimum (about 35–40 °C), then falls because enzymes denature. So rate at 50 °C is not higher than at 20 °C → FALSE P23 Q1c(i). In cold weather, temperature is the limiting factor → C P23 Q1a(vi).
  • Limiting factor: the factor in shortest supply, which controls the rate.

Experiments (Elodea / Hydrilla)

  • Count oxygen bubbles (or measure the volume of O2) given off by Elodea in a fixed time. Light: move the lamp nearer or farther. Temperature: water baths. CO2: add sodium hydrogen carbonate (NaHCO3).
  • Control = the same setup without the factor being tested. In P25 Q1a(iii) the experiment has water + sodium hydrogen carbonate and the control has only water → the factor is carbon dioxide (D).
  • Variables: independent = factor you change; dependent = bubbles / volume of O2 / rate; controlled = the other factors.

  • Sieve element: long living tubes with sieve plates that conduct food — compared with the oesophagus that passes food along P24 Q1a(v): B. Companion cell: controls the sieve element and supplies ATP. Phloem fibre: support. Phloem parenchyma: stores reserve food and conducts food P25 Q1a(iv): D.

Translocation: source and sink

  • Translocation = movement of the products of photosynthesis (sucrose) through the phloem P25 Q1b(i) P23 Q9c(i). It is driven by active loading of sugars into the phloem (needs ATP), after which water follows from the xylem by osmosis and pushes the sap to where it is used P24 Q1a(vi): C. So it needs water and energy → TRUE P23 Q1c(ii).
  • Source = where food is made or released: mature leaves (also storage roots/tubers in spring). Sink = where food is used or stored: roots, growing shoot tips, fruits, seeds, tubers P23 Q9c(ii). Leaves are a source because they make glucose by photosynthesis in excess of their own needs P25 Q4c(ii).

Ring (girdling) experiment

  • A complete ring of bark (including the phloem) is removed; the xylem stays. The part removed along with bark = phloem P24 Q1b(iv).
  • After a few months: swelling above the ring, because sugars coming down from the leaves cannot cross the gap and pile up P23 Q7d(i). Below the ring the bark and the roots do not get food, so they die; the leaves stay green for a time because water still rises in the xylem.
  • Conclusion: food is carried downward through the phloem, in the bark.

Water loss & wilting

  • On a hot sunny day leaf cells lose water by transpiration and become flaccid, so leaves droop, which reduces water loss P23 Q1b(i). The hormone abscisic acid closes stomata to reduce transpiration.

The digestive system

  • Path of food: mouth → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → large intestine → rectum → anus. Glands: salivary glands, liver (makes bile; the gall bladder stores it), pancreas.
  • 2024 figure: A = liver, B = stomach (check). 2025 figure: A = liver, B = stomach, C = pancreas, D = small intestine P25 Q2a.

Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori)

  • A spiral bacterium living in the stomach lining; it survives the acid by making urease (changes urea to ammonia). It causes gastritis, stomach (peptic) ulcers and raises the risk of stomach cancer P25 Q1a(ix): C P24 Q4d(i).
  • Why a concern in Bhutan: high rates of infection and of gastric cancer; spreads through contaminated food/water and shared utensils; the Ministry of Health uses triple therapy for 14 days and endoscopy for those 40 and above P23 Q6b(i).
  • Diagnosis: urea breath test or stool antigen test (also blood test, endoscopy with biopsy) P23 Q6b(ii) P24 Q4d(iii). Screening lets us treat early, preventing ulcers and cancer and stopping spread P24 Q4d(ii).

Healthy digestion & diet

  • Eat mindfully and chew thoroughly P24 Q1a(vii): D. Regular exercise improves blood flow and peristalsis. A regular eating schedule trains the gut to release juices on time, so digestion and absorption are steady P24 Q9b.
  • Constipation — prevent by: eating fibre (vegetables, fruits, whole grains), drinking plenty of water, regular exercise, regular toilet habits, less junk/processed food P23 Q8b.
  • Processed food contains additives, excess salt, sugar and fat → lifestyle diseases (obesity, diabetes, high blood pressure, kidney disease) → B P25 Q1a(xvii).

Natural selection

  • Steps: (1) variation in a population; (2) more offspring are born than can survive (competition); (3) individuals with favourable traits (best adapted) survive and reproduce — fitness = ability to survive and reproduce in a given environment P24 Q1a(xxii): D; (4) they pass the advantageous alleles on, so their frequency increases over time P24 Q1a(xxiv): A.

  • Peppered moths: soot from factories darkened trees, so dark moths were camouflaged and survived; white moths were eaten. A change in the environment can result in evolution → B P23 Q1a(xxiii).
  • Warming reduces snow, so black rabbits are better camouflaged on dark soil and thrive → C P25 Q1a(xiii). Mice graph: brown mice increase, white mice decrease → natural selection favours brown (camouflaged in the bushy habitat) P23 Q5d.

Hardy–Weinberg principle

  • In a large, randomly mating population with no mutation, migration, selection or drift, allele frequencies stay constant from generation to generation: p + q = 1 and p2 + 2pq + q2 = 1. If p = 0.7 then q = 0.3 (q2 = 0.09, 2pq = 0.42) → A P24 Q1a(xxv).
