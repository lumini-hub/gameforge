Chapter 1: Moments, Couples, Centre of Gravity, Equilibrium & StabilityAppears in Section A and Section B every year

1.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Moment of a force (torque)The turning effect of a force about a pivot. Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. Unit: N m. Bigger force or longer arm → bigger moment.
Principle of momentsWhen a body is in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments = sum of the anticlockwise moments about any point.
CoupleTwo equal, opposite, parallel forces acting at different points (not along the same line). Net force = 0, but they give a pure turning effect. Moment of couple = F × perpendicular distance between the forces. Examples: turning a steering wheel, a key, a tap, a bottle cap, a screwdriver, pedalling a bicycle.
Moment of a force vs coupleForce: one force about a pivot (opening a door by its handle, using a spanner). Couple: two forces, no pivot needed, net force zero.
Centre of gravity (C.G.)The single point where the whole weight of a body appears to act. Uniform square or rectangle: at the centre (a 6 cm × 6 cm square: (3, 3)).
Resultant force & equilibrantThe resultant is the single force equal to the net effect of all forces. The equilibrant is the force that balances the unbalanced forces (equal and opposite to the resultant).
Equilibrium of a bodyTwo conditions: (1) net force = 0 (no change in motion); (2) net moment = 0 (no turning).
Types of equilibriumStable: when slightly displaced the C.G. rises and the body returns (bottle standing on its base). Unstable: C.G. falls and the body moves further away (pencil on its tip). Neutral: C.G. stays at the same height; it stays in the new position (bottle on its side, a ball).
Stability depends on(1) Position of the C.G. — the lower, the more stable. (2) Area of the base — the larger, the more stable. A body topples when the vertical line through its C.G. falls outside its base.
Everyday usesLower C.G.: loaded lorries kept low, racing cars. Wide base: Buddha Dordenma statue, tripod, pyramids. Spreading arms or using a walking stick widens the support and keeps the line of gravity within the base.
FormulaUse
Moment = F × dwrench, door, see-saw, lever, torque
F1d1 = F2d2 (clockwise = anticlockwise)balanced beams / levers
Resultant = difference (opposite directions) or sum (same direction)forces on a body

Chapter 2: Gravitation, Free Fall & Terminal VelocitySection A and Section B in all three years

2.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Law of universal gravitationEvery body attracts every other body with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres: F = G m1m2 / r². G = 6.67 × 10−11 N m² kg−2.
Two factors(1) the masses of the two bodies, (2) the distance between them. Double both masses → F becomes 4×. Double the distance → F becomes ¼.
Weight and gW = mg. g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (≈ 10) on Earth and about 1.6 m/s² on the Moon. g does not depend on the mass of the falling body.
Free fallMotion under the action of gravity alone (air resistance negligible, e.g. a coin on the Moon, a hammer and a feather in the Apollo 15 demonstration). All bodies fall with the same constant acceleration g; velocity increases uniformly: v = g t (from rest).
Air resistance (drag)Opposes the fall; increases with speed and surface area. A sheet of paper falls more slowly than a paper ball of the same mass because its larger area gives a larger drag.
Terminal velocityAs speed grows, drag grows until drag = weight (driving force). Net force = 0 → acceleration = 0 → the body falls with a constant velocity. Before it: net force and acceleration decrease but are not zero.
MicrogravityIn space an astronaut's pen cannot write because the ink is not pulled to the nib by gravity.
FormulaUse
F = G m1m2 / r²gravitational force; to find a mass: m2 = F r² / (G m1)
W = m g    v = g t    h = ½ g t²    v² = 2 g hfree fall (from rest)

Chapter 3: Work, Energy, Power, Efficiency & Energy ResourcesCalculations and "design / justify" questions in all three years

3.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
WorkWork = force × distance moved in the direction of the force. Unit: joule (J). Zero work is done when there is no displacement (pushing a wall) or when the force is perpendicular to the displacement (carrying a book while walking horizontally, a runner completing one lap of a circular track: displacement is zero). Work is positive if the force acts in the direction of motion and negative if it opposes it.
EnergyCapacity to do work (J). Kinetic energy = ½ m v²; gravitational potential energy = m g h. Law of conservation: energy cannot be created or destroyed, only changed from one form to another.
PowerRate of doing work: P = W / t = F v. Unit: watt (W). The same work done in different times gives different powers.
EfficiencyEfficiency = (useful energy output / total energy input) × 100 %. It is always less than 100 % because some energy is wasted as heat and sound.
Energy efficiencyDoing the same job with less energy. Methods: efficient (5-star / LED) appliances, insulation, switching off, natural lighting, good design. Benefits: lower electricity bills, lower demand and imports, fewer emissions, resources conserved.
Voltage dropThe fall in voltage along wires because of their resistance (heat I²R is wasted). Minimising it (thick, short wires; good joints; higher-voltage transmission) saves energy and keeps machines at full efficiency and safe.
Energy resourcesRenewable (solar, wind, hydro) reduce dependence on fossil fuels and emissions. Bhutan depends on hydropower, which is low in winter, so solar adds energy security.
FormulaUse
W = F d    P = W/t = F v    KE = ½ m v²    GPE = m g hwork, power, energies
Efficiency = (output / input) × 100 %machines, appliances, see-saw, transformer

Chapter 4: Pressure, Pascal's Law & Hydraulic MachinesEvery year: at least one hydraulic calculation and several pressure MCQs

4.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
PressureForce acting perpendicularly per unit area: P = F / A. Unit: pascal (Pa) = N/m².
Pressure due to solidsDepends on (1) the force (weight) and (2) the area of contact. P ∝ F and P ∝ 1/A. Same force on a smaller area → larger pressure (sharp knife, nail, rocket nozzle). Larger area → smaller pressure (wide bag strap, camel's feet, wide foundations, tractor tyres).

Atmospheric / water pressure changeAs a bubble rises, the water pressure on it falls, so it expands.

Hydraulic machineSmall effort force F1 on a small piston (A1) gives pressure P = F1/A1, transmitted to a large piston (A2): F2 = F1 × A2/A1. It is a force multiplier (jack, brakes, lifts, excavators). Work in = work out, so the small piston moves a longer distance.
Gas in place of liquidPascal's law holds for any fluid, including gases, but a gas is compressible: part of the effort is used in compressing it, so it is unsuitable for hydraulic machines. Liquids (oil) are used.

TermMeaning / rule
Heat vs temperatureHeat is energy transferred (J); temperature shows how hot a body is and decides the direction of heat flow: from the hotter to the colder body. Two bodies can have the same temperature but different heat contents (a bucket and a cup of water).
Thermal equilibriumWhen two bodies in contact reach the same temperature there is no net heat flow.

Applications of high s.h.c. of waterCoolant in car radiators, hot-water bags, fomentation, coastal climate, watering plants before frost (water releases heat slowly and latent heat when freezing).

Chapter 6: Current Electricity — Ohm's Law, Resistance & Heating EffectV–I graphs and a current / power calculation appear every year

6.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Electric current (I)The rate of flow of electric charge in a circuit: I = Q / t. Unit: ampere (A). Conventional current flows from the positive to the negative terminal; electrons flow from the negative (higher electron concentration) towards the positive terminal.
Potential difference (V)Energy given per unit charge; unit: volt (V).
Resistance (R)Opposition to current: R = V / I; unit: ohm (Ω). R depends on length (R ∝ L), cross-sectional area (R ∝ 1/A: a thicker wire has less resistance), material and temperature.

Heating effectH = I² R t: depends on current, resistance and time. A filament lamp has a thin, high-resistance tungsten filament, so it becomes white/red hot.
Electrical powerP = V I = I² R = V² / R. Current drawn: I = P / V.

7.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Motor effectA current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force. Direction by Fleming's left-hand rule: Thumb = Force (motion), First finger = Field (N→S), Second finger = Current. Reversing the current (or the poles) reverses the direction of the force/rotation; reversing both leaves it unchanged. Used in the electric motor.
Electromagnetic inductionAn emf (and current) is induced in a coil whenever the magnetic flux through it changes (magnet moved in or out of a solenoid, a conductor cutting field lines). GeneratorRotating a coil in a magnetic field induces an emf. AC generator: slip rings and carbon brushes → alternating current. DC generator: a split-ring commutator → direct current. The emf is greatest when the coil is parallel to the field (cutting lines fastest).
TransformerChanges AC voltage using two coils on an iron core. Vs/Vp = Ns/Np. Step-up: Ns > Np; step-down: Ns < Np (mobile charger). Ideal (100 %): VpIp = VsIs, so voltage up means current down. Works only with AC.
Why not 100 % efficientHeat in the coils (I²R, copper loss), eddy currents in the core, hysteresis loss, flux leakage, and sound/vibration.
Step-up for transmissionThe same power P = V I is sent at a very high voltage, so the current is small and the heat loss I²R in the long lines is small. A step-down transformer then lowers the voltage for homes.

Wave equationv = f λ. For EM waves c = 3 × 108 m/s in a vacuum, so frequency ∝ 1/wavelength: when frequency increases, wavelength decreases.

Chapter 9: Radioactivity & Nuclear EnergyA decay equation and an alpha/beta/gamma question appear every year

9.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Nucleus notationAZX: A = mass (nucleon) number = protons + neutrons; Z = atomic (proton) number. Isotopes have the same Z but different A.
RadioactivityThe spontaneous emission of radiation (α, β, γ) from unstable nuclei, which lose particles/energy to become stable (they do not "receive" extra protons or neutrons).
Alpha (α)Helium nucleus 42He (2 protons + 2 neutrons), charge +2, heavy. Highest ionising power, lowest penetration (stopped by paper/skin). Bent towards the negative plate.
Beta (β)A fast electron 0−1e (from a neutron changing to a proton), charge −1, light. Medium ionising and penetration (stopped by a few mm of aluminium). Bent strongly towards the positive plate.
Gamma (γ)High-energy EM wave, no charge or mass. Lowest ionising power, highest penetration (reduced only by thick lead/concrete). Not deflected by electric or magnetic fields.
Decay equationsAlpha: A decreases by 4, Z by 2. Beta: A unchanged, Z increases by 1. Gamma: A and Z unchanged. Totals of A and of Z must balance on both sides.
UsesTreating cancer (gamma), sterilising equipment, medical tracers (iodine-123 for the thyroid: gamma passes out through the body and has low ionising power), smoke detectors, tracers in industry.
Hazards & safetyIonising radiation damages cells (burns, cancer, mutations). Precautions: handle with long tongs, store in lead-lined containers, wear protective clothing and a radiation badge (dosimeter), keep a distance and minimise the exposure time, no eating or drinking in the area, proper disposal of waste.
Nuclear energyEnergy released by nuclear fission (heavy nuclei such as uranium split, in reactors) or fusion (light nuclei join, in stars). It is not the same as radioactivity, which is the spontaneous decay of unstable nuclei (uncontrolled and not used to generate power directly).

Chapter 10: Space Exploration & the UniverseAn "explain / compare / justify" question comes from this chapter every year

10.1 Must-know summary

TopicKey points
Orbiter spacecraftEnters and stays in orbit around a planet or moon; studies it for a long time, maps it and sends data (needs fuel to slow down and be captured). E.g. Mars orbiters, Chandrayaan orbiter.
Flyby missionPasses a planet once without orbiting or landing, collecting data briefly; needs little fuel, can visit several bodies. E.g. Voyager 1 (Jupiter, Saturn), New Horizons.
Rover / landerLands and moves on the surface, collects soil and rock samples and searches for signs of life (Perseverance, Curiosity). Cheaper and safer than humans, but slow and limited.
Human space explorationAstronauts travel (Apollo 11, 1969): flexible and can make decisions and bring samples back, but very expensive and risky to life. Its main impact: technological advances with wide uses on Earth (satellites, medical devices, materials).
Robotic explorationUse of autonomous or remote-controlled spacecraft to study celestial bodies.
Pros and consAdvantages: more knowledge of the universe, minerals and resources, new technology, inspires young scientists. Disadvantages: very expensive (economy diverted, possibly leading to poverty), risk to astronauts, space debris.
Life on other planetsNeeds liquid water, suitable temperature, an atmosphere and an energy source (conditions we know from Earth). Habitable-zone planets, water on Mars and Europa suggest possibility, but no life has been found yet.
Big Bang theoryThe universe began about 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding. Evidence: galaxies moving apart (red shift) and the cosmic microwave background. Critics point out that it does not explain what existed before or caused the bang, the singularity cannot be tested, and observations such as very old structures or dark matter/energy are not fully explained.

StarsStars shine because of nuclear fusion of light elements (hydrogen → helium) in their cores. A massive star ends as a supernova (a huge explosion that can outshine a galaxy) leaving a neutron star or black hole.
Origin of the solar systemNebular hypothesis (Fig 10.1): a cloud of gas and dust collapses, flattens into a spinning disc, forms the Sun and, by accretion, planets.
