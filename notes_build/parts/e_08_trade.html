Tariff = tax on imported goods. A trade deficit and harm to local industries from imports can be tackled by export promotion [[P24 Q1a(vii): C]]. Bhutan has largely free trade with India and a mix of restrictions with other countries.

Benefits and problems of economic cooperation

Benefits: larger markets and market accessibility, foreign exchange, consumer sovereignty (production of goods according to consumers’ choices and preferences [[P24 Q1c(iii): a]]) [[P25 Q1d(v): FALSE]], technology and investment, better relations. Problems: domestic industries dislodged, dumping of inferior goods, political interference and loss of sovereignty, spillover of crises, WTO decisions slow (Doha round). Selling through e-commerce platforms such as Amazon and Flipkart increases market accessibility [[P25 Q1a(xvi): B]].

Solved questions — Section B

Do international relationships always benefit a country? No. (1) Dependence: Bhutan relies on a few partners (India) so their problems reach us (spillover); (2) domestic industries are hurt by cheap imports and dumping of inferior goods; (3) political and cultural interference, loss of sovereignty and brain drain. Benefits (markets, investment, technology) exist but depend on the terms. [3] P25 Q4a: the SAARC members are Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan and Sri Lanka (8) [2]. P24 Q4a: the organisation around the Bay of Bengal is BIMSTEC [1]; one function: promote trade, investment and economic integration of the Bay of Bengal region / research new areas of cooperation [1]. Trade relationships boost growth by: (1) bigger markets for exports, so production, jobs and foreign exchange rise; (2) technology, skills and foreign investment come with trade partners; (3) competition and specialisation raise quality and efficiency and give cheaper inputs and wider choice. [3] Rules for a new organisation of Bhutan, India, Nepal and Bangladesh: (1) gradual removal of tariffs and trade barriers to give free movement of goods and services; (2) equal rights and special help for smaller, less developed members (Bhutan, Nepal) so that benefits are shared equitably, with respect for each country’s sovereignty; (3) a fair way to settle disputes with transparent, regularly reviewed rules (and shared standards for environment and labour).
