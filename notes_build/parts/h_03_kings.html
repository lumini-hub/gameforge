The Wangchuck dynasty

  • Jigme Namgyel (Trongsa Poenlop, 1825–81) strengthened Trongsa’s power. His son Ugyen Wangchuck united the country, mediated for Britain (1904) and was elected first Druk Gyalpo on 17 Dec 1907 (National Day).
  • 2nd King Jigme Wangchuck (1926–52). 3rd King Jigme Dorji Wangchuck (1952–72), “Father of modern Bhutan”: National Assembly (1953), end of serfdom, Five Year Plans from 1961, High Court, UN membership 1971.
  • The Third Five Year Plan (1971–76: communications, irrigation, health, education, agriculture) was chaired by Prince Jigme Singye Wangchuck [[P25 Q1a(ii): B]].

The Fourth Druk Gyalpo

  • Born 11 Nov 1955 at Dechencholing, Thimphu. He ascended the throne on 24 July 1972 aged 16; he was formally enthroned/crowned on 2 June 1974 [[P24 Q1a(i): A]]. On 16 June 1972 he was appointed Trongsa Poenlop and received the red scarf (Kabney) → TRUE [[P24 Q1d(i)]] (check).
  • Gross National Happiness (GNH) — wellbeing above GDP. Decentralisation: Dzongkhag Yargay Tshogdu (1981) and Gewog Yargay Tshogchung (1991) — the mechanism to involve people in grassroots decision-making [[P25 Q1c(i): c. Fourth DG]].
  • Conservation: 60% forest cover, protected areas. Modernisation: hydropower, free education and health.
  • Operation All Clear (Dec 2003): after years of talks, His Majesty personally led the army to remove militant camps (ULFA, NDFB, KLO) from southern Bhutan — with minimal damage. [[P25 Q1e(iii)]] [[P24 Q6c]]
  • Democracy: started drafting the Constitution (2001); abdicated on 9 Dec 2006 in favour of the Crown Prince — before the people demanded change.

Solved questions

Why the Fourth Druk Gyalpo is a “Selfless Leader”: (1) in 2003 he led the troops personally from the front, risking his life for the nation; (2) he voluntarily abdicated in 2006 and gave up power; (3) he gave democracy and the Constitution as a gift, not under pressure; (4) he made GNH the aim — wellbeing of people over personal wealth; (5) he decentralised power to the people and travelled to remote villages, serving rather than ruling. Speech: FIVE key aspects of his leadership/legacy: (1) GNH philosophy; (2) decentralisation and democracy (DYT/GYT, Constitution); (3) environmental conservation — 60% forest cover for all time; (4) national security — Operation All Clear 2003; (5) socio-economic development & culture — hydropower, schools, hospitals and preservation of tradition — capped by his selfless abdication. Inspiration from his selfless leadership in 2003: (1) put the nation before self — I should serve my school/community before my comfort; (2) lead by example and be present in difficult times; (3) prepare carefully and choose peace first (talks before force) — solve conflicts patiently; (4) be ready to sacrifice time and personal gain for others’ safety and happiness. TWO lessons from the 2003 military operation: (1) dialogue and patience first, force as last resort — militants were given time to leave peacefully; (2) strong leadership with careful planning and unity — the King led from the front, trained the forces and united the people, so the mission succeeded with few casualties.
