9.1 Must-know summary

ElementDetails
Author & genreLois Lowry, American author. The Giver (1993) won the Newbery Medal. Genre: dystopian science fiction for young adults — a society that looks perfect but hides a terrible cost.
SettingA future community controlled by Sameness: no colour (as people see it), no weather, hills or snow, no pain, no choice. Time: the days before and after the Ceremony of Twelve in December. Beyond the community lies "Elsewhere".
Point of viewThird-person limited: we see events through Jonas's thoughts and feelings only — this makes us share his confusion, fear and discoveries.
Main charactersJonas — 11 turning 12, thoughtful, pale-eyed, chosen as the Receiver of Memory. The Giver — the old Receiver who holds all memories; wise, sad and lonely. Father (a Nurturer) and Mother (Department of Justice), sister Lily. Asher (friend, playful) and Fiona (friend, red hair). Gabriel (Gabe) — a baby with pale eyes who sleeps badly. The Chief Elder — leader who announces the assignments. Rosemary — the earlier Receiver-in-training who asked for Release.
Plot in briefJonas is anxious about the December Ceremony. He is selected as the Receiver, the one person who keeps the community's memories. The Giver transmits memories: snow, sled, sunshine, colour, love, joy — and pain, war and death. Jonas learns that "Release" means killing: he sees his own father release a baby twin. Gabriel is to be released, so Jonas decides to escape with him. Their journey is cold and hungry; at the end they reach a hill with snow and a sled and see a house with lights and hear music — an open ending.
Themesfreedom and choice vs control • memory, pain and joy — true humanity needs both • individuality vs Sameness • love and relationships • cost of a "perfect" society • courage and sacrifice.

9.2 Key terms of the community

TermMeaning
SamenessThe community's system: everyone is alike, all differences and strong feelings are removed to avoid conflict and pain.
Receiver of Memory / The GiverThe one person who holds the memories of the past. The Receiver is trained by the Giver and advises the Elders.
AssignmentThe job given by the Committee of Elders at the Ceremony of Twelve, based on observation of each child.
ReleaseA polite word for killing (by injection). Used for the old, the weak, rule-breakers and some newchildren.
ElsewhereThe place outside the community where released people are said to go.
StirringsThe first signs of adult feelings, suppressed daily with pills.
Precise languageThe rule to use the exact word and avoid emotion (e.g. apprehensive not frightened).
Family unitTwo adults (a couple matched by the Committee) and two children (one boy, one girl); not a natural family.
Seeing beyondJonas's ability to see something that others cannot (first sign: the apple changes; later, colours).

2024 D-Q1(b)(i)What would be the advantages of living in an ordered society without family and loving relationships? Suggest and explain any TWO. [5]

Firstly, such a society is safe and peaceful. Everyone follows the same rules, so there is no poverty, hunger, crime or war, as in Jonas's community. Secondly, without strong emotional bonds, people do not suffer grief, jealousy or heartbreak. Decisions about jobs and spouses are made by the Elders, so there is no competition or wrong choice and life is calm and predictable.

2024 D-Q1(b)(ii)Do you like the ending of the novel? Justify your stand with TWO reasons linked to ideas stated in the novel. [5]

Yes, I like the ending. Firstly, it is hopeful: Jonas and Gabriel reach a snowy hill, a sled, lights and music, which recall the warm memory of a family Christmas and suggest freedom and love. Secondly, the open ending lets readers imagine the future and shows Jonas's courage in risking everything to save Gabriel and return the memories to the people.

2024 D-Q2(a)The concept of individual choice in The Giver is different from the one we understand and practise in our society. Explain THREE benefits if the concept practised in the novel is applied in our society. [10]

In the novel, the Committee of Elders makes the important choices for everyone, such as jobs, spouses and family. If this concept were applied in our society, there could be three benefits.

First, every person would get a job that suits his or her abilities. Jonas is observed for years before he is made the Receiver, so nobody is unemployed or chooses a wrong career, and talent is used well.

Second, society would be orderly and safe. When people follow the same rules and nobody competes for wealth or power, there would be less crime, fewer accidents and less conflict, as in Jonas's community where there is no hunger or war.

Third, people would be free from stress and regret. Young people would not worry about choosing a career or a partner, and nobody would blame himself for a wrong decision. Life would be calmer and more predictable.

However, these benefits come at the cost of freedom and feelings.

2024 D-Q2(b)The novel "The Giver" can be classified as science fiction. Identify any THREE elements of science fiction and elaborate each with examples from the novel. [10]

Science fiction imagines a future world shaped by science and technology. The Giver has at least three such elements.

The first element is advanced technology and control of nature. The community has Climate Control, so there is no snow, rain or hills. People use pills to suppress "Stirrings", and the Release of the old and babies is done by an injection.

The second element is a controlled, dystopian society. The Committee of Elders decides jobs, spouses and children, and Sameness removes differences, colour and choice. What looks like a perfect community hides cruelty, because the "Release" is really killing.

The third element is speculative scientific ideas. The Giver transmits memories to Jonas through the touch of his hands, and Jonas gains the "capacity to see beyond", such as seeing the colour red in the apple.

These elements make the novel a warning about a future without freedom.

2025 D-Q1(b)(i)Lowry's use of foreshadowing contributes to the development of the plot. Mention ONE example of foreshadowing from the novel and explain how it helps the readers in understanding the events better. [5]

One example is the apple. While playing catch with Asher, Jonas notices the apple change, although he cannot explain it. This hints early that Jonas has a special ability to see beyond. When he is later chosen as the Receiver and starts seeing colour, readers understand that the apple was the first sign of his gift, so his selection feels logical rather than sudden.

2025 D-Q1(b)(ii)"Needless to say, he will be released, the voice had said, followed by silence." Is the "release" system in Jonas's community justifiable? List and explain any TWO reasons. [5]

No, the release system is not justifiable. Firstly, it is murder disguised by a polite word: Jonas sees his own father inject a baby twin, which violates the basic right to life. Secondly, the community uses it to remove the weak, the old and rule-breakers without telling people the truth, so people lose their freedom, dignity and compassion.

2025 D-Q2(a)How does "Sameness" affect Jonas's ability to form personal connections in the community? Use examples from the novel to illustrate its impact on his relationships and his ability to connect deeply with others. [10]

Sameness removes strong feelings and differences, so it makes deep connections almost impossible for Jonas.

In his family unit, the evening ritual of telling feelings is polite and shallow. When Jonas asks his parents whether they love him, they laugh and say that the word is meaningless and that he must use precise language. Because they cannot feel deeply, Jonas cannot share his thoughts with them.

His friends also cannot understand him. Asher and Fiona see no colour, so after Jonas starts seeing red he cannot share it, and he feels lonely and different. Even his feelings for Fiona are treated as "Stirrings" to be controlled with pills.

Only after Jonas receives memories does he know love, and he forms real bonds with the Giver and with Gabriel, whom he risks his life to save. Thus Sameness blocks personal connection, and memory restores it.

2025 D-Q2(b)How is the use of precise language shown to instill control and obedience in the community? Support your points using evidence from the text. [10]

In Jonas's community, language is carefully controlled so that people cannot think or feel beyond the rules.

First, children are corrected whenever their words are not exact. Jonas is told that "frightened" is the wrong word and he should say "apprehensive", and he is told to avoid exaggerated words like "starving". This trains people to hide their feelings and to accept the system.

Second, harsh realities are hidden behind polite words. "Release" means killing, and "Elsewhere" hides the truth about where people go. Because the words sound gentle, nobody questions what happens.

Third, rules and announcements are communicated by a speaker, and everyone must reply politely with set phrases, such as apologising and accepting apologies. This produces habit and obedience rather than thought.

Thus precise language limits thought, emotion and rebellion.
