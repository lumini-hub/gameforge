Key ideas

  • Copyright = the exclusive legal right given to the creator to print, publish, perform, film or record a work and to authorise others to do so [[P24 Q1b(iii): copyright]]. It applies automatically to books, music, photos, software and websites.
  • Plagiarism = using someone else’s work or ideas as your own. Acknowledging the source by citing it avoids plagiarism [[P24 Q1b(ii): plagiarism]].
  • Using copyrighted work legally: get permission/consent from the owner, use work with a suitable licence (e.g. Creative Commons), use it under fair use/educational use with credit, or use public-domain material. So a physics project may use a journal’s work with the author’s consent → TRUE [[P25 Q1c(iv)]].
  • To use an online song in a video follow the rules by making sure the use matches the licence (credit alone is not enough) → D [[P25 Q1a(i)]].

Creative Commons (CC) licences

[[fig:i_cc_licences|The CC licences from most open to most restricted.]]
  • Creative Commons licences let others use, share and adapt a work under conditions chosen by the creator [[P24 Q1a(x): B]]. The creator keeps the copyright; CC does not transfer ownership to the public domain, so “CC lets creators waive their copyright completely” is FALSE [[P24 Q1c(v)]] (check) (only CC0 waives rights).
  • BY = give credit (attribution). NC = non-commercial only. SA = share alike. ND = no derivatives.
  • CC BY-NC: others can remix/share the song but not for profit [[P25 Q1a(xiii): B]].

Copyright vs Creative Commons

CopyrightCreative Commons
RuleAll rights reserved; you need permission for every useSome rights reserved; permission is given in advance with conditions
Effectprotects the creator’s income and control, but limits sharingencourages sharing, remixing and education while still crediting the creator

Citing sources (APA 7th edition)

  • In-text citation: placed inside the text, e.g. (Dorji, 2020) [[P25 Q1b(iii): in-text]]. Reference list: full details at the end.
  • Book format: Author, A. A. (Year). Title in sentence case, in italics. Publisher. Only the first word and proper names are capitalised; no city in the most recent (7th) edition.
Dorji, T. (2020). Public health challenges in Bhutan. Bhutan Health Trust Fund.
         (title in italics)        -> P25 Q1a(iv): C
Smith, J. (2021). The digital age: Psychological impacts of social media.
         HarperCollins Publishers. -> P24 Q1a(v): B (no place of publication)

Ethical online behaviour

  • Cyberbullying / fake accounts (Pema’s fake profile): can lead to school discipline (warning, suspension), legal action under the cyber/ICT laws, deleting the account, and harm to the victim [[P25 Q3a]].
  • Good digital citizens: respect privacy, think before posting, give credit, report abuse, use strong passwords, and do not copy files without permission.
