  • Absent in a prokaryote: mitochondria [[P25 Q1a(i): C]]. Unique to prokaryotes: the nucleoid (and plasmid) [[P25 Q3b]]. A ciliated organism with a visible nucleus is eukaryotic [[P23 Q1a(i): B]].
  • Why prokaryotes matter: decomposers (recycle nutrients), nitrogen fixers, gut bacteria (digestion, vitamin K and B), curd and fermentation, bioremediation, biogas.

The cell membrane & types of transport

[[photo:d_cell_membrane|The cell membrane: a phospholipid bilayer with proteins (fluid mosaic model).]]
  • The membrane is a phospholipid bilayer with proteins (fluid mosaic model); it is selectively (semi-) permeable: it lets some substances pass and blocks others.
  • Diffusion: movement of molecules from high to low concentration (down the gradient); no energy. E.g. smell of ema datsi spreading in a room [[P24 Q1a(ii): D]]; O2 and CO2 exchange.
  • Facilitated diffusion: diffusion of larger or charged molecules (glucose, ions) through carrier / channel proteins; down the gradient; no ATP [[P25 Q1d(ii): a]].
  • Osmosis: movement of water from a region of low solute (high water) concentration to high solute concentration through a semipermeable membrane [[P25 Q1a(ii): D]].
  • Active transport: movement against the gradient (low → high) using carrier proteins and energy (ATP): e.g. mineral ions into root hairs, glucose uptake, Na+–K+ pump.

[[fig:b_tonicity|Effect of hypotonic, isotonic and hypertonic solutions on animal and plant cells.]] [[photo:d_tonicity|Real red blood cells in hypertonic, isotonic and hypotonic solutions.]]
  • Salting fish, olives or vegetables: the hypertonic brine draws water out of bacteria, which die of dehydration [[P24 Q1b(i): hypertonic]].
  • Wilting in salt water: the soil water becomes hypertonic, so roots lose water by osmosis and leaves lose turgor [[P25 Q6a]].
  • Soaking seeds: water enters the seed by osmosis/imbibition; the seed coat softens and the embryo activates, so germination is faster [[P23 Q6a]].
  • Wax on fruits reduces water loss (transpiration) and membrane transport, so fruits stay fresh longer: statements I and III → D [[P23 Q1a(iv)]].

Experiments you must know

[[fig:b_osmoscope|Potato osmoscope: before (A) and after (B). Water enters the sugar solution, so its level rises.]]
  • Potato osmoscope: a cavity is scooped in a potato and filled with sugar (or salt) solution; a pin marks the initial level; the potato stands in a beaker of water. After about 1 hour the level in the cavity rises because water moves by osmosis from the water (high water concentration) through the potato tissue (semipermeable) into the concentrated solution.
  • Control: a boiled (dead) potato, or a cavity with water instead of sugar. Keep the same potato size, volume of liquid, temperature and time.
  • Potato cubes in sugar solutions: cube gains volume in dilute solutions (hypotonic), no change in the isotonic one, and shrinks in strong solutions (hypertonic).
