Intermediate / final goodsintermediate (semi-finished) are processed further (flour for bread); final goods are bought by consumers or producers (as investment)
Public goods / private goodspublic: provided by the government, non-excludable (cannot stop anyone) and non-rival (one person’s use does not reduce another’s) [[P24 Q1a(v): C]]; private: owned by individuals, must be bought

Circular flow of income

  • Shows the flow of payments and receipts between sectors: households, business firms, government and the rest of the world. Two-sector model: households sell factor services and receive factor income (wage, rent, interest, profit = national income); they spend it on goods and services (consumption expenditure).
  • Real flow = factor services and goods and services; money flow = payments and receipts (wages, expenditure). Households providing labour to a firm is a real flow [[P24 Q1a(xx): D]]; paying wages or buying groceries are money flows.
  • With a capital market: saving flows from households to the capital market and investment flows to firms. Equilibrium when saving = investment; if saving > investment, demand and economic activity fall.

Domestic product vs national product

GDP (domestic)GNP (national)
Meaningvalue of all final goods and services produced within the boundary of a country in a yearvalue of final goods and services produced by the citizens, at home or abroad; = national income
Whocitizens and foreigners working inside the countryonly nationals, wherever they work
RelationGNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad

GDP per capita = national income ÷ total population (average income of a person). Growth is measured by the rate of GDP [[P25 Q1a(xx): D]]; per capita income [[P24 Q1c(i): d]].

Economic growth vs development

Economic growthEconomic development
Meaningincrease in GDP / per capita income (short run: rate of GDP; long run: sustained rise in per capita income with structural change)wider change: better quality of life, education, health, employment, shelter, sustainable use of natural resources
Scopeone indicator (income)many indicators (literacy, life expectancy, poverty)
Natureignores pollution, depletion and equityconcerned with the environment and equity
  • Growth is necessary but not sufficient: growth can occur without development, but development needs growth; growth is a subset of development.
  • Kuznets’ five features of modern growth: rising saving and investment; structural transformation; rising factor productivity; rising per capita income; use of modern science and technology.

Solved questions — short answers and Section B

Can economic growth guarantee development? No. (1) Growth only means a rise in GDP or income; it does not show how income is shared (inequality can rise) or the state of health, education and happiness; (2) growth may harm the environment (pollution, depletion) so quality of life can fall. Growth is necessary, but development also needs equity and sustainability. Rapid growth with sustainable development: (1) use clean and renewable energy (hydropower, solar) and green technology, with environmental impact assessment for projects; (2) conserve the environment — keep 60% forest cover, recycle and use resources efficiently — and follow the GNH approach of balancing material and non-material wellbeing. [2] No, GDP and GNP are not the same: (1) GDP counts production within the country’s boundary by both citizens and foreigners, while GNP counts production by the citizens inside or outside the country; (2) they differ by net factor income from abroad: GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad (income earned abroad by nationals minus income taken home by foreigners).
