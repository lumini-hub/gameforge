Economic indicators

  • An economic indicator is a statistic about the economy: unemployment, current account, income inequality, GDP per capita, education, longevity.
  • Unemployment: people in the labour force without a paid job but willing and available to work; lower unemployment = better performance.
  • Current account: exports and imports of goods and services in a year; a favourable (surplus) account = better performance.
  • Income inequality: gap in income distribution; lower inequality = better performance.
  • Human Development Index (HDI): scale 0 to 1, three dimensions: longevity (life expectancy at birth; health, spending on healthy food and exercise [[P25 Q1b(iv): longevity]]), education (adult literacy and school enrolment) and GDP per capita. Broader than GDP.

Pro-family economic approach

A policy that puts the wellbeing of families first, believing productivity rises when families are supported. Examples: paid maternity leave, higher minimum wage, paid day-care, free school supplies, equal access to education, training and health care, income enough to save.

Gross National Happiness (GNH)

  • Idea of the Fourth King (1970s); in the Constitution; a holistic approach balancing material and non-material wellbeing; “GNH is more important than GDP.” Development has been aligned with GNH since the 9th Five Year Plan. GDP measures economic prosperity; GNH measures overall wellbeing [[P24 Q1a(xiv): D]].

Economic issues

  • Global: growth instability, inflation, global oil shocks, trade tensions, recession (short term); global inequality and poverty, exhaustion of resources, climate change (long term). Local: unemployment, high rent, rural–urban migration, non-performing loans, import dependence.
  • Spillover effect: an economic condition in one country spreads to another, e.g. a rise in petrol prices in India raises prices in Bhutan [[P24 Q1a(vi): C]].
  • Brain drain = movement of talented, skilled people to another country for education and jobs [[P25 Q1a(xix): B]].

Solved questions — Section A and short answers

vi: ACC and the Office of the Attorney General exist to improve good governance (A). xiv: yoga (health, wellbeing), counselling and volunteering at an old age home build bonds between people → community vitality (B). xviii: urban non-food spending (Nu 37,496) is higher than rural (Nu 24,746) mainly because the cost of living is high in urban areas (D) (check). xix: brain drain (B). vi: higher prices in Bhutan caused by Indian petrol prices → spillover effect (C). xiv: GNH emphasises a holistic approach while GDP measures economic prosperity (D). Can a big investment in education achieve the pro-family goals? Largely yes. (1) Education gives children and adults skills and jobs, raising family income and savings, the aim of a pro-family approach; (2) free supplies, equal access and better literacy support every family member and reduce inequality. (It must be joined with health care, child care and jobs to meet all goals.) About 64,000 Bhutanese (9%) have migrated abroad. Will it benefit the country? Mostly not. (1) Brain drain and labour shortage: skilled workers and youth leave, so farms and services lack workers and the country loses its investment in their education; (2) the population and rural villages shrink and the gap between regions grows. (A benefit is remittances and skills learned abroad, but they do not replace the loss.) “Major economic problems are mostly in urban areas”: I agree. (1) Higher unemployment (urban 10.4% against rural 3.4% in 2022) because of rural–urban migration; (2) high rent and cost of living, congestion, pollution and a bigger gap between rich and poor. (Rural areas have poverty and poor services too.)

Solved questions — Section B

Unemployment in urban areas was 10.4% and in rural 3.4% (2022). Reasons: (1) rural–urban migration — many job seekers move to towns, so there are more job seekers than jobs; (2) skills mismatch and preference for white-collar jobs — educated youth wait for office jobs; (3) rural people are self-employed in farming and are counted as employed; urban jobs depend on a small private sector.
