The digestive system

[[fig:b_digestive|Human digestive system with the glands: salivary glands, liver (A), gall bladder, stomach (B), pancreas, small and large intestine.]] [[photo:d_digestive|Human digestive system (labelled diagram).]]
  • Path of food: mouth → pharynx → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine (duodenum, jejunum, ileum) → large intestine → rectum → anus. Glands: salivary glands, liver (makes bile; the gall bladder stores it), pancreas.
  • 2024 figure: A = liver, B = stomach (check). 2025 figure: A = liver, B = stomach, C = pancreas, D = small intestine [[P25 Q2a]].

[[fig:b_digest_flow|Digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats: enzymes and end products.]]
  • Carbohydrate (rice, bread): starch → maltose → glucose (the final product that cells absorb) [[P24 Q1b(ii)]]. Protein (beef, beans): → peptides → amino acids. Fat: → fatty acids + glycerol.
  • Small intestine adaptations (part D): very long and coiled; wall folded into villi with microvilli → huge surface area; thin (one-cell) lining; rich capillary network and lacteal to carry absorbed food [[P25 Q2a(ii)]] [[P25 Q1c(ii): TRUE]].
  • Pancreatic disease (chronic pancreatitis) → reduced digestion of carbohydrates, proteins and fats → B [[P25 Q1a(v)]]. Gastritis (less gastric juice) → decreased breakdown of proteins in the stomach → C [[P25 Q1a(xxiii)]].

Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori)

[[photo:d_hpylori|Helicobacter pylori bacteria on the stomach lining.]]
  • A spiral bacterium living in the stomach lining; it survives the acid by making urease (changes urea to ammonia). It causes gastritis, stomach (peptic) ulcers and raises the risk of stomach cancer [[P25 Q1a(ix): C]] [[P24 Q4d(i)]].
  • Why a concern in Bhutan: high rates of infection and of gastric cancer; spreads through contaminated food/water and shared utensils; the Ministry of Health uses triple therapy for 14 days and endoscopy for those 40 and above [[P23 Q6b(i)]].
  • Diagnosis: urea breath test or stool antigen test (also blood test, endoscopy with biopsy) [[P23 Q6b(ii)]] [[P24 Q4d(iii)]]. Screening lets us treat early, preventing ulcers and cancer and stopping spread [[P24 Q4d(ii)]].

Healthy digestion & diet

  • Eat mindfully and chew thoroughly [[P24 Q1a(vii): D]]. Regular exercise improves blood flow and peristalsis. A regular eating schedule trains the gut to release juices on time, so digestion and absorption are steady [[P24 Q9b]].
  • Constipation — prevent by: eating fibre (vegetables, fruits, whole grains), drinking plenty of water, regular exercise, regular toilet habits, less junk/processed food [[P23 Q8b]].
  • Processed food contains additives, excess salt, sugar and fat → lifestyle diseases (obesity, diabetes, high blood pressure, kidney disease) → B [[P25 Q1a(xvii)]].

Solved questions

(i) A = liver; C = pancreas. (ii) Structural adaptation of D (small intestine): inner wall has villi and microvilli to increase the surface area for absorption (or: long, thin walled, rich blood supply). (iii) HCl in the stomach (B): kills bacteria in food, provides the acid medium for pepsin and activates pepsinogen into pepsin. (a)(i) Part A = liver, Part B = stomach (check). (ii) Bile emulsifies fats into small droplets and makes the food alkaline. (iii) Mucus protects the stomach lining from the acid and pepsin. (b)(i) Rice (starch begins digestion by salivary amylase). (ii) Beef (and beans) — protein is absorbed as amino acids. (c)(i) Mr B takes more carbohydrate at every meal; the excess is converted to glycogen and then stored as fat, so he gains more weight. (ii) No — excess carbohydrate causes obesity, diabetes, tooth decay and heart disease; eat a balanced diet. (d) See H. pylori notes above. (i) Exercise increases peristalsis and blood flow to the gut, so food moves and is digested faster. (ii) A gallstone blocks the bile duct, so less bile reaches the small intestine — fat is not emulsified, so poorly digested; pain and fatty stools. (iii) Comment: chewing gum makes saliva and gastric juice flow with no food, so too much can cause acid, gastritis and bloating (swallowed air); a little sugar-free gum after meals is acceptable. (iv) Regular meals let the gut release enzymes and bile at set times, give time to digest and absorb fully and prevent overeating and acidity. Flow chart: mouth (not digested) → stomach (digested) → small intestine (digested) → villi (absorbed). (i) Food = protein (digestion begins in the stomach). (ii) Final product: amino acids. (iii) Enzymes: pepsin (stomach), trypsin and peptidases (small intestine). Q4a(ii): fat-digesting enzyme in the stomach = gastric lipase. Q6d: Partly agree.
