Key ideas

  • Excretion = removal of metabolic wastes: urea, uric acid, creatinine, excess water and salts (kidneys → urine; skin → sweat; lungs → CO2). Urinary system: kidney → ureter → urinary bladder → urethra.
  • The nephron is the structural and functional unit of the kidney (about one million in each kidney). Urine is formed from blood by the nephron → TRUE [[P23 Q1c(v)]].

Parts of the nephron

PartJob
Glomerulus (capillary knot)High-pressure filtration of blood
Bowman’s capsuleCup that collects the filtrate (primary urine)
Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)Reabsorbs all glucose and amino acids, most Na+ (active) and water (passive) [[P24 Q1d(iii): e]]
Loop of HenleMakes a salt gradient in the kidney so water can be reabsorbed: the descending limb is permeable to water [[P25 Q1b(iii): descending]]; ascending limb takes out salts [[P24 Q1d(iv): f]]
Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)Fine control: Na+ (aldosterone) and water (ADH) reabsorbed; secretion of urea, uric acid, creatinine, K+
Collecting ductTakes urine to the pelvis; water reabsorbed under ADH [[P24 Q1d(v): h]] (check — g if your text uses DCT)

How urine is formed (3 steps)

[[fig:b_urine_flow|Urine formation: filtration, selective reabsorption and secretion (flow chart of P23 Q5a).]]
  • 1 Ultrafiltration in the glomerulus: water, glucose, amino acids, urea and salts pass into the capsule; blood cells and big proteins stay in the blood.
  • 2 Selective reabsorption: useful substances (all glucose, most water, salts) return to the blood. The composition graph shows glucose in the filtrate but none in urine → glucose is reabsorbed [[P23 Q1a(xii): C]]. Urea is more concentrated in urine (not reabsorbed).
  • 3 Tubular secretion: urea, uric acid, creatinine and excess ions are added from the blood into the tubule. Urine then flows through the collecting duct, ureter and bladder.
  • Hormones: aldosterone → reabsorption of sodium in the DCT; ADH (antidiuretic hormone) → reabsorption of water in the DCT / collecting duct [[P23 Q5a(i)]]. When the body has little water, more ADH is released, more water is reabsorbed, so a small volume of concentrated urine is passed [[P23 Q5a(ii)]].

Kidney health

  • Low physical activity → obesity and hypertension → damage to the kidney → C [[P25 Q1a(viii)]]. Healthy lifestyle choices can prevent or delay kidney failure in many cases → D [[P24 Q1a(x)]].
  • Unhealthy practices that harm the kidney: too much salt (salted butter tea, pickles, processed food), too much sugar and red meat, drinking too little water, alcohol and smoking, overuse of painkillers, obesity and high blood pressure, diabetes.
  • Treatment of kidney failure: dialysis or kidney transplant.
