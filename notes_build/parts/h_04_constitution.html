The Constitution of the Kingdom of Bhutan

  • Drafted from 2001 by a committee led by Chief Justice Sonam Tobgye after nationwide consultation; draft shared in 2005; enacted 18 July 2008. It is the supreme law of modern times, drafted under the Royal Command (compare Kathrim).
  • The Druk Gyalpo is the Head of State and symbol of unity of the Kingdom and the people; he does not preach any religion or represent any party — “the embodiment of the Constitution” [[P25 Q1a(v): D]] (check).
  • Three branches: Legislature (Parliament: National Council + National Assembly), Executive (Lhengye Zhungtshog), Judiciary. Commanding that a bill be deliberated in Parliament is a legislative matter, not an exercise of executive role → FALSE [[P24 Q1d(vi)]] (check).
  • GNH is a principle of State policy: four pillars (sustainable and equitable development, environment conservation, culture, good governance). GNH assesses development by material and spiritual progress together → TRUE [[P25 Q1d(i)]].

Environment — Article 5

  • Every Bhutanese is a trustee of the Kingdom’s natural resources and environment for present and future generations → TRUE [[P24 Q1d(v)]]; it is the fundamental duty of every citizen to protect the environment, conserve biodiversity and prevent ecological degradation (noise, visual and physical pollution), by adopting eco-friendly practices and policies.
  • The Government must protect, conserve and improve the environment, prevent pollution, and keep at least 60% of the land under forest cover for all time. Parliament enacts environmental legislation.
  • Flow chart box X [[P25 Q1a(xi)]]: the paper’s chart lists Trustee, Government responsible, Legislation by Parliament; X = declared Protected Areas / forest-cover commitment → A (check). Bhutan has about 51% of its land in protected areas and is carbon negative.
  • To strengthen the environment an individual should educate family and friends on its importance for sustainability [[P24 Q1a(viii): D]].

Solved questions

What makes Bhutan’s Constitution different? (1) It was drafted on the initiative of the King, not demanded by revolution, after consulting the people; (2) GNH is a guiding principle of State policy; (3) it requires 60% forest cover for all time and makes every citizen an environmental trustee; (4) monarchy rules: the Druk Gyalpo retires at 65 and can be removed by Parliament; also religious institutions and personalities remain above politics. Citizens’ responsibilities (Art. 5.1): (1) protect the natural environment and conserve Bhutan’s rich biodiversity as a trustee for future generations; (2) prevent all forms of ecological degradation including noise, visual and physical pollution by following environment-friendly practices and policies. THREE strategies for protecting biodiversity: (1) keep and expand forest cover (60%) by plantation and strict forest laws; (2) strengthen protected areas and biological corridors and community forestry; (3) promote clean energy/electric vehicles, waste management and eco-tourism, with awareness and environment impact assessment of projects.

11 · Civil Society, Community & Everyday Life

Key ideas

  • Civil Society Organisations (CSOs) are non-profit, non-government groups that work for public welfare, e.g. RENEW (women), Bhutan Kidney Foundation, Tarayana, RSPN, Draktsho. The BKF logo represents a Civil Society Organisation [[P24 Q1a(ix): B]].
  • CSOs are meant to be non-political and registered under the CSO Act (2007) to keep neutrality and public trust.
  • A victim of domestic abuse receives support from RENEW / the National Commission for Women and Children (NCWC) and the police Women and Child Protection Unit [[P24 Q1b(v)]] (check wording).
  • Self-help mechanisms in villages — neighbours helping with house building, rice planting and harvest by sharing labour — build community vitality and cut costs. [[P25 Q5c]] [[IMG 4 photos]]

Solved questions

Should CSOs engage in political activities? @@No@@ — (1) the CSO Act requires CSOs to be non-political; (2) political involvement would destroy neutrality and public trust, divide society and shift focus from welfare services to party interests. Public awareness campaign on traditional self-help. Theme: “Together We Build — Self-Help Strengthens Communities.” Four messages: (1) “Many hands make light work” — sharing labour (house building, farming) saves money and time; (2) “Help today, help tomorrow” — reciprocity builds trust and security in need; (3) “Keep our culture alive” — self-help is part of Bhutanese tradition and identity; (4) “Strong communities, happy lives” — it supports GNH and fights loneliness in town life.

Community vitality is one of the nine GNH domains: trust, volunteering and mutual help (self-help, woola).

Culture & identity

  • Bhutan’s identity rests on language (Dzongkha), national dress (gho/kira), Driglam Namzha, dzong architecture, festivals (tshechus) and Buddhism.
  • Preserving culture (youth): wear the national dress, learn Dzongkha and traditional arts (Zorig Chusum), attend festivals, record elders’ stories (oral history), respect elders and protect heritage sites.
  • The Constitution (Art. 4) asks the State to preserve, protect and promote the cultural heritage, including monuments, places and objects of artistic or historic interest, and living traditions.

Likely questions

  • Why should CSOs stay out of politics? neutrality, trust, focus on welfare.
  • How does self-help help communities? saves cost, builds trust, keeps traditions, helps in emergencies.
  • How can you contribute to GNH? volunteer, protect nature, preserve culture, practise good citizenship.
