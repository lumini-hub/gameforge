1 · The Universe & the Solar System
P25 Q1a(iii),b(iii),c(i),Q5b · P23 Q1a(i),d(iii),Q2d,Q4e

Key ideas

  • Universe = all space, matter and energy. Galaxy = billions of stars held by gravity (our Milky Way). Light-year = distance light travels in one year (≈ 9.46 trillion km).
  • Big Bang Theory: about 13.8 billion years ago the universe began as a single, extremely hot, dense point, then expanded and cooled; matter formed stars and galaxies. Put forward by the Belgian priest Georges Lemaître (1927) [[P25 Q1a(iii): A]]; Edwin Hubble (1929) showed galaxies are moving apart (not the theorist of the Big Bang).
  • Expansion of the universe means a wider gap between galaxies (space itself stretches; galaxies are not “new”) [[P23 Q1a(i): B]].
  • Evidence: (1) red shift; (2) cosmic microwave background radiation (afterglow); (3) proportion of hydrogen (~75%) and helium (~25%).
  • Red shift = light from distant galaxies is stretched to longer (redder) wavelengths because they are moving away; the farther the galaxy, the bigger the shift → the universe is expanding → it must have been smaller and denser in the past. [[P23 Q2d]]
  • Solar Nebula Theory (Kant, Laplace): the solar system formed ~4.6 billion years ago from a rotating cloud of dust and gas (the nebula) [[P23 Q1d(iii): nebula]]. Gravity pulled the cloud in; it spun faster, flattened into a disc; the centre became the Sun.
  • Accretion: dust grains stuck together → planetesimals → they collided and merged into larger protoplanets → planets. The process is called accretion [[P25 Q1c(i)]]. The Big Bang does not explain planets forming from dust and gas → 2025 b(iii) FALSE.
  • Planets: inner terrestrial (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) – small, rocky, because heat drove light gases away; outer giants (Jupiter, Saturn gas giants; Uranus, Neptune ice giants).

Solved questions

Why is the Solar Nebula Theory widely accepted? (1) It explains why all planets orbit the Sun in the same direction and almost the same plane (they formed from one spinning disc). (2) It explains the two kinds of planets — small rocky ones near the hot Sun, large gas/ice giants in the cold outer region. (3) Telescopes (and space probes) observe young stars surrounded by discs of dust and gas, and the theory fits the age of meteorites and Moon rocks (~4.6 billion years). Red shift: meaning — the shift of light from a receding galaxy to longer (red) wavelengths, like the drop in pitch of a siren moving away. It explains that galaxies are moving away from each other, so the universe is expanding — evidence that it began from a single point (Big Bang). Structure of the Earth

  • Crust (thin, solid; continental thicker, oceanic thinner) → mantle (~2,900 km, hot flowing rock) → outer core (liquid iron-nickel) → inner core (solid).
  • Lithosphere = crust + rigid top of mantle, broken into plates. These plates “look like wooden blocks floating over a weak, ductile region”, the asthenosphere [[P23 Q1a(v): D]]. Tuzo Wilson (1960s) gave the idea of plates of continental and oceanic crust.
[[fig:earth_layers|Layers of the Earth.]]

Continental drift & plate tectonics

  • Alfred Wegener (1912): all continents were once joined as the supercontinent Pangaea (“all land”) ~200–300 million years ago, which split and drifted into present positions [[P25 Q1c(iv): Pangaea]]. Pangaea first split into Laurasia (north) and Gondwana (south).
  • Evidence: jigsaw fit of coasts; same fossils on separate continents; matching rock layers/mountains; glacial deposits in warm lands.
  • Plate boundaries: convergent (collide: fold mountains, trenches, volcanoes, earthquakes), divergent (move apart: rift valleys, mid-ocean ridges), transform (slide past: earthquakes).
  • Himalayas: the Indian plate moved north and converged with the Eurasian plate 50–70 million years ago; the sediments of the Tethys Sea between them were crumpled and folded upward, forming young fold mountains [[P23 Q1a(iv): C]] [[P25 Q1a(vi): B]] [[P23 Q1d(iv): fold mountains]]. Convergent movement presses both sides of the boundary and folds the rock layers. The plates are still pushing, so the Himalayas keep rising and Bhutan has earthquakes.
  • Proof the Himalayas were under the sea: (1) marine fossils (ammonites, shells) found in Himalayan rocks; (2) sedimentary rocks such as limestone and shale formed on the sea bed lie at high altitude; (3) salt deposits and the sea-floor rocks (ophiolites) found there. [[P23 Q2e]]

Geological Time Scale (GTS)

  • A chronological system dating Earth’s rock strata and history. Order from oldest: Precambrian → Palaeozoic → Mesozoic → Cenozoic [[P25 Q5a]].

Solved questions

Chronological order: i. Precambrian → iii. Palaeozoic → ii. Mesozoic → iv. Cenozoic (i, iii, ii, iv). FOUR events of the Cenozoic era (66 mya to today): (1) the Himalayas and Alps rose as the Indian plate collided with Eurasia; (2) mammals spread and became the dominant animals (Age of Mammals); (3) Ice Ages (Pleistocene) covered large areas and sea level rose and fell; (4) modern humans appeared; also the continents reached their present positions and grasslands spread. If the continents stayed as one land mass (Pangaea) — TWO consequences: (1) very dry continental interior far from the sea: vast deserts and extreme hot summers/cold winters; (2) one huge ocean, shorter coastline → less coastal land, fewer marine habitats and less rainfall; also species would mix and some become extinct, and there would be no Himalayas or Indian Ocean as now. (Any two.) The Himalayas were once under the sea — TWO evidences: (1) marine fossils such as ammonites and shells are found in the high rocks; (2) the rocks are marine sedimentary rocks (limestone, sandstone) deposited on the Tethys sea bed and later lifted when India collided with Eurasia.
