  • Mitosis uses somatic cells to create genetically identical cells → D [[P25 Q1a(xix)]]. Meiosis I is called reduction division because the homologous chromosomes separate, so the number falls from 2n to n [[P25 Q4e]].
  • Anaphase (mitosis / meiosis II): the sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles as spindle fibres shorten → diagram C (elongated cell with chromatids at the poles) [[P24 Q1a(xvi)]] (check). The figure with chromosomes (each of two chromatids) moving to the poles = Anaphase I → C [[P25 Q1a(xi)]] (check).
  • Chromosomes in a cell stuck at prophase I (egg in a girl): 46 chromosomes and 92 chromatids (DNA already copied) → D [[P24 Q1a(xv)]].
  • Haploid vs diploid: haploid (n, gametes) has one set of chromosomes, no pairs; diploid (2n, body cells) has two sets in homologous pairs [[P25 Q5e]]. Body cells carry maternal and paternal information; sex cells carry one set → C [[P24 Q1a(xvii)]].

Why gametes differ: variation

[[fig:b_crossing|Crossing over in prophase I: non-sister chromatids exchange segments (answer B in P23 Q1a(xix)).]]
  • Crossing over (exchange of segments between homologous chromosomes), independent assortment of chromosomes and random fertilisation all make offspring different. After the crossing-over shown in P23 Q1a(xix), each chromosome carries a piece of the other → B.
  • Meiosis increases genetic variation, mitosis does not → TRUE [[P25 Q1c(v)]].

Mendel’s laws & vocabulary

  • Gene = unit of heredity; allele = form of a gene. Homozygous (same alleles: TT, tt), heterozygous (different alleles: Tt) [[P24 Q1b(v)]]. Genotype = alleles; phenotype = visible trait.
  • Law of dominance: when two contrasting alleles are present, the dominant allele masks (expresses over) the recessive one [[P25 Q1a(xii): B]] [[P23 Q9e]]. A recessive allele expresses only in the homozygous form [[P25 Q1d(v): e]]. Law of segregation: the two alleles of a gene separate when gametes form.
  • Dominant parent × recessive parent giving all dominant offspring → the dominant parent is homozygous dominant → B [[P24 Q1a(xx)]]. Homozygous red × homozygous white (red dominant) → 100% red → B [[P25 Q1a(xxv)]].

Beyond Mendel; sex-linked traits

[[fig:b_dominance|Complete dominance, incomplete dominance and codominance.]]
  • Incomplete dominance (A): the heterozygote is intermediate (black × white → grey). Codominance (B): both alleles show fully (e.g. black and white patches; blood group AB). Unlike Mendelian inheritance, no allele completely hides the other [[P25 Q2c]].
  • Sex: XX = female, XY = male. Haemophilia and colour blindness are recessive genes on the X chromosome (sex-linked) [[P23 Q5e]]. They are more common in males, who have only one X, so a single recessive allele shows; females need two [[P25 Q8c(ii)]] [[P23 Q1a(xxii): C]] [[P24 Q1a(xviii): B]].
  • Carrier female × unaffected male: 50% of sons are affected [[P24 Q1a(xix): C]]. A couple with 50% haemophilic children and the rest carriers = normal father and diseased mother (sons affected, daughters carriers) → B [[P23 Q1a(xxi)]].
