Key ideas

  • Cloud computing = using servers, storage and software over the internet instead of on your own computer; you pay for what you use. Cloud services provide services over the internet → TRUE [[P25 Q1c(iii)]].
  • Cloud storage (e.g. Google Drive) keeps files on remote servers instead of a local device [[P25 Q1b(i): servers]]. You can open them from any device with internet.

Benefits of cloud storage

  • Lower upfront cost: no need to buy servers and hard disks; pay a small monthly fee (pay-as-you-go) [[P24 Q2b(i)]].
  • Scalability: add or remove storage when the business grows or in busy seasons, instantly [[P24 Q2b(ii)]].
  • Backup and recovery: copies are kept in several data centres, so data can be restored after hardware failure, fire or theft [[P24 Q2b(iii)]].
  • Access and sharing: work from anywhere, share links, edit together; automatic updates and reduced maintenance.
  • Risks: needs internet, security/privacy concerns, monthly cost, depends on the provider.

Solved questions

Two reasons to advise cloud storage: (1) no costly servers to buy and maintain — the provider does it and the company pays only for what it uses; (2) data is backed up and accessible from anywhere, so staff can work remotely and data is safe if a computer is damaged. Choose SaaS (or IaaS to remove servers): for increased efficiency and reduced on-site server maintenance a school IT team should use SaaS because the provider manages the servers, software and updates and the school just uses the applications. (IaaS removes the hardware but the team still has to manage the OS and software, so it does not reduce work as much.) Two features of Google Forms: (1) different question types (multiple choice, short answer, scale) so the feedback is organised and easy to answer; (2) automatic collection of responses into a Google Sheet with summary charts, so data is gathered and analysed without manual typing. (Also: sharing by link, required questions, response validation.) (i) The business does not need to buy servers, disks or build a server room; it rents storage and pays monthly. (ii) Scalability: storage can be increased or decreased quickly as inventory grows or in seasonal periods, paying only for what is used. (iii) Backups are stored off-site in several data centres, so after hardware failure, fire or theft the data can be restored quickly and business continues. (i) Create a Google Doc, click Share, add classmates’ emails with Editor access; everyone edits at the same time, uses comments and suggestions and the version history records changes. (ii) Google Drive is the cloud storage to store, organise, share and sync files from any device. (iii) Slides: real-time collaboration, ready templates, themes, animations and transitions; can be presented or shared online (any two). P24: a company that wants to develop and run applications without managing hardware or software updates → PaaS (D). P25: a school wanting control and high security → private cloud (A).
