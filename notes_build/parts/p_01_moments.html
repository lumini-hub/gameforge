1.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Moment of a force (torque)The turning effect of a force about a pivot. Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. Unit: N m. Bigger force or longer arm → bigger moment.
Principle of momentsWhen a body is in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments = sum of the anticlockwise moments about any point.
CoupleTwo equal, opposite, parallel forces acting at different points (not along the same line). Net force = 0, but they give a pure turning effect. Moment of couple = F × perpendicular distance between the forces. Examples: turning a steering wheel, a key, a tap, a bottle cap, a screwdriver, pedalling a bicycle.
Moment of a force vs coupleForce: one force about a pivot (opening a door by its handle, using a spanner). Couple: two forces, no pivot needed, net force zero.
Centre of gravity (C.G.)The single point where the whole weight of a body appears to act. Uniform square or rectangle: at the centre (a 6 cm × 6 cm square: (3, 3)).
Resultant force & equilibrantThe resultant is the single force equal to the net effect of all forces. The equilibrant is the force that balances the unbalanced forces (equal and opposite to the resultant).
Equilibrium of a bodyTwo conditions: (1) net force = 0 (no change in motion); (2) net moment = 0 (no turning).
Types of equilibriumStable: when slightly displaced the C.G. rises and the body returns (bottle standing on its base). Unstable: C.G. falls and the body moves further away (pencil on its tip). Neutral: C.G. stays at the same height; it stays in the new position (bottle on its side, a ball).
Stability depends on(1) Position of the C.G. — the lower, the more stable. (2) Area of the base — the larger, the more stable. A body topples when the vertical line through its C.G. falls outside its base.
Everyday usesLower C.G.: loaded lorries kept low, racing cars. Wide base: Buddha Dordenma statue, tripod, pyramids. Spreading arms or using a walking stick widens the support and keeps the line of gravity within the base.
FormulaUse
Moment = F × dwrench, door, see-saw, lever, torque
F1d1 = F2d2 (clockwise = anticlockwise)balanced beams / levers
Resultant = difference (opposite directions) or sum (same direction)forces on a body

2023 Q2(a) || A 6 m plank is pivoted 2 m from Dema's end. Dema (450 N) sits on that end. Where must Pema (250 N) sit to balance? || Clockwise moment = anticlockwise moment: 450 N × 2 m = 250 N × d ⇒ d = 900 / 250 = 3.6 m from the pivot on the other side (0.4 m from the end of the plank, which is 4 m long on that side). Bottle on its side: neutral equilibrium — if rolled, its C.G. stays at the same height and it stays in the new position. (Unstable equilibrium: a body that, when displaced, has its C.G. lowered and moves further away.) 2023 Q4(a) || State the principle of moments. || When a body is in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about a point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point. 2023 Q6(a) || Which truck has lesser stability? Why? || Truck B: its tall, covered load raises the centre of gravity and the base is narrow compared with its height, so a small tilt can bring the line of gravity outside the base. Truck A has a low flat body and a low C.G. 2023 Q7(a)(i) || How can the mechanic increase the torque of the wheel wrench? || Use a longer wrench (or add a pipe to the handle) and push at the far end, perpendicular to the handle: torque = F × d increases with d. 2023 Q7(a)(ii) || How do larger steering wheels help drivers of heavy vehicles? || A large wheel gives a larger distance between the two hand forces, so the moment of the couple is larger for the same force: the driver needs less effort to turn the heavy vehicle. 2023 Q9(a) || Which stand-and-clamp set-up is most stable? || B. The flask is clamped low (lowest centre of gravity) and lies directly above the base. Car: 2000 − 2000 = 0 N. 2023 Q9(b)(ii) || Which body is at equilibrium? || The car: the resultant force is zero (it is at rest or moving at constant velocity). The plane has a resultant of 13 000 N, so it accelerates. 2024 Q2(c) || ONE difference between the moment of a force and the moment of a couple, with an example of each. || Moment of a force: a single force turning a body about a pivot; M = F × perpendicular distance from the pivot; e.g. pushing a door at its handle. Give TWO relationships for equilibrium. || (1) Net force = 0: the upward force equals the sum of the downward forces (V = U + W). (F = 800 N at 2 m, W = 1800 N at 1 m) || Moment of the effort = 800 × 2 = 1600 N m. Moment of the load = 1800 × 1 = 1800 N m. 1600 < 1800, so no, he cannot move the rock.
