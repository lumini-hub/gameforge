Ecosystem

  • An ecosystem = biotic (living: plants, animals, microbes) + abiotic (non-living: sunlight, water, soil, air, temperature) factors interacting in a place [[P23 Q1a(xv): C]]. Biotic–abiotic interaction: a tree grows in the soil [[P25 Q1a(xviii): C]]. All the different forms of life together make biodiversity [[P23 Q1b(iii)]].
  • Producers (autotrophs) are always at the base of a food pyramid [[P24 Q1a(xiv): A]]; then herbivores, carnivores; decomposers recycle nutrients.

[[fig:b_biodiv|Biodiversity and ecosystem health reinforce each other (draw this for P24 Q5c).]]
  • A healthy ecosystem needs biodiversity: it keeps food webs stable, recycles nutrients, supports pollination, and makes the ecosystem resilient and resistant to invasive species and diseases [[P25 Q3f]]. Poaching is not an example of this relationship → A [[P24 Q1a(xi)]].
  • Biodiversity hotspot = a region with very high numbers of species, many found nowhere else (endemic), that is under serious threat. There are 34 hotspots; the Eastern Himalaya (with Bhutan) is one [[P23 Q2c]].
  • Endangered species = a species at very high risk of extinction in the wild (tiger, red panda, golden langur) [[P25 Q8e]]. Bhutan supports many because of its diverse habitats → TRUE [[P25 Q1c(iv)]]. The tiger graph falls steeply because of poaching and habitat loss → C [[P25 Q1a(x)]].

Conserving biodiversity in Bhutan

  • The Constitution: at least 60% forest cover for all time; a network of national parks, wildlife sanctuaries and biological corridors (about half the country); laws against hunting and wildlife trade; community forests; eco-tourism with the Sustainable Development Fee; awareness programmes [[P25 Q3c]] (any two).
  • Will Bhutan remain a hotspot? Yes, if it keeps the forest law and protected areas and controls climate change, invasive species, poaching, hydropower and road building [[P23 Q2c(ii)]].

Threats & invasive species

  • Threats: habitat destruction and deforestation, pollution, poaching, invasive species, climate change, high dams (loss of biodiversity, loss of habitats) [[P23 Q1a(xvi,xvii)]] (check: the paper copy bolds option C for xvii). To protect biodiversity we should minimise destruction of habitat, pollution and invasive species [[P23 Q1a(xvi): C]] (check).
  • Deforestation, competency-based measure: work with local communities on agroforestry and give standing forests an economic value → D [[P24 Q1a(xiii)]]. Kitchen waste turned into manure by decomposers (figure) = recycling → A [[P24 Q1a(xii)]] (check).
  • Invasive species = a non-native species that spreads quickly and harms native species, the ecosystem and the economy [[P25 Q6d(i)]] [[P24 Q5b(i)]]. Bhutan examples: Lantana camara, Ageratina adenophora (crofton weed), polka dot plant, Mikania.

Health: HIV, COVID-19 and lifestyle

  • HIV spreads by unprotected sex, sharing needles or shaving blades, infected blood, mother to child; hugging does not spread it → A [[P23 Q1a(xiv)]]. Reduce stigma: awareness/education campaigns, testing with counselling, laws and workplaces that do not discriminate, support groups, involve people living with HIV [[P25 Q6e]].
  • COVID-19 (novel coronavirus): being vaccinated does not make mass gatherings without masks safe → FALSE [[P24 Q1c(iii)]].
  • Beneficial bacteria in the body: gut bacteria help digestion, make vitamins and crowd out harmful germs [[P25 Q4a]].
