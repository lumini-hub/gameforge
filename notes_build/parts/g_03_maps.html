Topographic maps

  • A topographic map shows the natural and man-made features of an area in detail: relief (contours, spot heights), drainage, vegetation, settlements, roads, trails, boundaries. Survey of Bhutan maps are at 1 : 50,000.
  • Map numbering (India & adjacent countries series, IAC): degree sheet (e.g. 78) → divided into 16 sheets lettered A–P (1° × 1°) → each lettered sheet divided into 16 numbered 1–16 (15′ × 15′, the 1:50,000 map). A map is written e.g. 78M/3 (Mongar) or 78M/11 (Trashigang). The numbering of topographic maps is not the same in every country → 2025 b(i) FALSE.
  • Conventional signs and symbols (colours and small pictures) [[P23 Q1e(vii)]]: blue = water, green = vegetation, brown = contours, black = man-made features, red = roads/settlements. They are important because: (1) they give a universal language so any user can read any map; (2) they save space and avoid clutter of words, so more information is shown clearly; (3) they let us identify features and routes quickly.

RF → statement scale: divide the denominator by 100,000 to get km per cm (denominator in cm ÷ 100,000 cm per km).

Grid references & contours

  • Four-figure reference = a grid square (e.g. 2030: easting 20, northing 30). Six-figure = an exact point: easting (2 digits + tenth) then northing (2 digits + tenth), e.g. 405502. Rule: “along the corridor, then up the stairs” — read the eastings (left→right) first, then the northings (bottom→top).
  • Contours join points of equal height. Contour interval = vertical gap between two contours (read from the map’s margin; every fifth is a thicker index contour). Close = steep; wide = gentle; V pointing uphill = valley; V pointing downhill = spur; spot height = a point with its height printed.
  • Drainage patterns: dendritic (tree-like), trellis (parallel main rivers with right-angled tributaries), radial (outward from a peak), parallel (steep uniform slope).

Measuring the length of a river

  1. Lay a thread carefully along every bend of the river on the map.
  2. Straighten it and measure with a ruler → map length (cm).
  3. Multiply by the scale: at 1:50,000, length (km) = cm × 0.5. Example: 8.6 cm × 0.5 = 4.3 km. [[P23 Q1e(iv)]] (map)

Survey and geospatial tools

  • Plane table survey: equipment = plane table (drawing board), tripod stand, alidade, spirit level, plumbing fork and plumb-bob, compass, drawing sheet, pins.
  • GPS (satellite positioning), GIS and Google Earth (satellite images, 3-D terrain, measuring tools).

Types of maps

  • Physical (relief), political (boundaries), thematic (rainfall, population), topographic (detailed, large scale), cadastral (land ownership; Bhutan thram maps).
