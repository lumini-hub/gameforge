8.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Transverse vs longitudinalTransverse: vibration perpendicular to the direction of travel (light, all EM waves, X-ray, radio, water ripples). Longitudinal: vibration parallel to the direction of travel, made of compressions and rarefactions (sound). Sound needs a medium; EM waves do not.
Wave equationv = f λ. For EM waves c = 3 × 108 m/s in a vacuum, so frequency ∝ 1/wavelength: when frequency increases, wavelength decreases.
EM waves (common properties)Transverse; travel in a vacuum at the speed of light; carry energy; can be reflected, refracted, diffracted; uncharged (not deflected by fields).
DiffractionWaves bend around obstacles or spread through openings, most when the wavelength is comparable to or larger than the obstacle. Radio waves (long λ) bend around hills; sound is heard around corners and from the next room.
Analogue vs digitalAnalogue: continuous values (sine wave), used in radio/TV broadcasting; affected by noise. Digital: only 0 and 1 (square wave); noise-free regeneration, easy storage and processing, secure, high bandwidth; used in computers, mobile phones, blood-pressure monitors.
ModulationPutting the information (input signal) on a carrier wave. AM: the amplitude of the carrier varies with the signal. FM: the frequency varies and the amplitude is constant.
Propagation of radio wavesGround waves (low frequency) follow the Earth's surface; sky waves (short-wave) are reflected by the ionosphere to far places; space waves (TV, FM, microwaves) pass through the ionosphere (satellite links) or travel in a straight line of sight between towers.
Wireless technologiesBluetooth (about 10 m), Wi-Fi (microwaves), mobile networks (microwaves), infrared remote controls (line of sight), satellite links (microwaves).
Wired: optical fibre vs copperFibre carries light by total internal reflection: very low signal loss, huge bandwidth/data, no electromagnetic interference, light, secure, does not corrode. Copper loses signal and picks up interference.
WaveMain usesHarm / precaution
Radioradio and TV broadcasting, mobile communication, radio telescopesvery low energy; harmless
Microwavesmobile phones, Wi-Fi, satellite and radar links, microwave ovensheat body tissue; limit phone use, keep transmitters away from homes
InfraredTV remote, night-vision and thermal photography (not scattered easily; objects emit it), heating, greenhouse effect (keeps Earth warm), medical therapyburns at high intensity
Visiblevision, photography, optical fibre—
Ultravioletsterilising, counterfeit-note detector (fluorescence), treating neonatal jaundice (phototherapy), vitamin Dskin burns/cancer, eye damage: sunscreen, goggles
X-raysmedical imaging (bones, strokes, tumours), airport securityionising: avoid unnecessary exposure; lead aprons; harmful to a foetus
Gammakilling cancer cells, sterilising equipment, medical tracers (iodine-123)ionising, most penetrating: lead shielding, limit time and keep distance

8.3 Solved questions — Section B

2023 Q2(c)(i) || A radiation X is focused on the bulb of a clinical thermometer and the mercury rises rapidly. Name X. || Infrared radiation (heat radiation). 2023 Q2(c)(ii) || ONE property demonstrated. || It has a heating effect (and it can be focused/refracted by a lens like light). 2023 Q2(d) || Why is a digital signal preferred to an analogue signal? || A digital signal is less affected by noise and can be regenerated without loss, can carry more information, is easy to store, process and encrypt (secure). 2023 Q3(e) || Waves A and B are modulated. Which is amplitude modulated? || Wave A: its amplitude varies in step with the input signal while its frequency stays constant. In wave B the frequency changes with a constant amplitude (frequency modulation). 2023 Q5(f) || TWO similarities between visible light and radio waves. || Both are electromagnetic waves and transverse; both travel at 3 × 108 m/s in a vacuum and need no medium (they also carry energy and can be reflected, refracted and diffracted). 2023 Q6(d) || Explain long-distance communication with the key words (caller's phone, transmitter, satellites, receiver, receiver's phone) and identify the wave. || See Fig 8.5: the caller's phone changes sound into an electrical signal → the transmitter (tower) sends it as radio/micro waves to a satellite → the satellite amplifies and retransmits it to the receiver (tower) → the receiver's phone changes the signal back into sound. The wave used: microwaves (radio waves). 2023 Q7(e) || Compare and contrast gamma rays and hard X-rays. || Differences: gamma rays come from the nucleus (radioactive decay), X-rays are produced outside the nucleus (fast electrons striking a metal target); gamma rays generally have higher energy/frequency and penetrate more. Similarities: both are EM waves, transverse, travel at c, uncharged, ionising and harmful to living cells. 2023 Q9(e) || Why are pregnant women advised not to take X-rays unless required? || X-rays are ionising radiation that can damage the cells and DNA of the rapidly growing foetus, causing mutations, birth defects or cancer. 2023 Q9(f) || Optical fibre is better than copper cable. Agree? One reason. || Agree: an optical fibre loses very little signal over long distances and carries much more information (larger bandwidth); it is also free from electromagnetic interference. 2024 Q3(b) || Overview of waves X and Y; why Y for BBS radio? || Wave X passes through the ionosphere into space (a space wave, e.g. satellite/TV/microwaves; travels in a straight line). Wave Y is reflected back by the ionosphere to the Earth (a sky wave, short-wave radio). BBS uses Y because the reflected waves can reach far, mountainous places beyond the horizon where direct line-of-sight is blocked. 2024 Q5(e) || TWO differences between radio waves and gamma rays. || Radio waves: longest wavelength, lowest frequency and energy; produced by oscillating charges in aerials; harmless, used in communication. Gamma rays: shortest wavelength, highest frequency and energy; emitted by unstable nuclei; ionising and harmful, very penetrating. 2024 Q7(b) || Should we be cautious about cell phone radiation? || Yes, it is sensible to be careful. Cell phones use microwaves, which are non-ionising but can heat tissue; the long-term effect of prolonged exposure is not fully known and many people (children especially) use phones for long periods. Precautions: limit use, use hands-free/speaker, keep the phone away from the body and do not sleep next to it. 2024 Q7(d) || Why are cell phone transmitters on hill tops? || Microwaves travel in straight lines (line of sight) and have short wavelengths that diffract very little, so hills and buildings block them. A high position clears the obstacles and covers a wider area. 2024 Q8(a) || Which wave does a counterfeit-money detector use? Why? || Ultraviolet rays: they make the special inks and security threads on real notes fluoresce (glow), which is invisible in ordinary light. 2024 Q9(e) || How do radio waves help communication when a mountain lies between the transmitter and receiver? || Radio waves have a long wavelength, so they diffract around the mountain and can also be reflected by the ionosphere, reaching the receiver behind the mountain where microwaves or light would be blocked. 2025 Q2(d) || How is the EM spectrum organised? What happens to wavelength as frequency increases? || It is arranged by increasing frequency (and energy), i.e. decreasing wavelength: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays (Fig 8.1). As the frequency increases, the wavelength decreases (c = f λ with c constant). 2025 Q3(d) || How does diffraction of radio waves affect propagation over long distances and around obstacles? || Radio waves have long wavelengths, comparable to hills and buildings, so they bend (diffract) around obstacles and along the curve of the Earth, so signals reach places behind hills and far away (sky waves reflected by the ionosphere reach even farther). 2025 Q6(e) || Cell phone: type of signal (1) between the satellite and tower, (2) between the mobile and the person. || (1) digital (square pulses); (2) analogue (the phone changes the digital signal back into the analogue sound wave for the listener). 2025 Q7(e)(i) || Which row shows the nature of light, sound and X-rays? || Row 2: light transverse, sound longitudinal, X-rays transverse. 2025 Q7(e)(ii) || How do you distinguish longitudinal and transverse waves? || By the direction of vibration compared with the direction of travel: in transverse waves the particles vibrate perpendicular to it (crests and troughs); in longitudinal waves they vibrate parallel to it (compressions and rarefactions). Also, longitudinal waves (sound) need a medium (Fig 8.2). 2025 Q8(d) || Minimise prolonged exposure to EM waves A and B (A is between gamma and ultraviolet; B between infrared and radio). || A = X-rays: avoid unnecessary scans, wear lead aprons, keep the exposure time short and stay behind shielding. B = microwaves: do not stay near transmitters, use phones with hands-free and for short times, keep damaged microwave ovens away/repaired. 2025 Q9(f) || Copper or optical fibre for a weak TV signal? TWO reasons. || Optical fibre: (1) very low signal loss (attenuation) over long distances so the signal stays strong; (2) large bandwidth carries many channels with good quality; also free from interference and secure.
