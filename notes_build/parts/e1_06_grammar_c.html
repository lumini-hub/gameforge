13.1 Direct → Indirect (reported) speech

ChangeRule
Reporting verbsaid (that) / told (someone) / asked / ordered / advised. Remove the quotation marks and add that.
PronounsI → he/she • we → they • my → his/her • our → their (changes according to the speaker and listener)
Tense (one step back)if the reporting verb is in the past — see the diagram below.
Time & place wordsnow → then • today → that day • tomorrow → the next day • yesterday → the day before • here → there • this → that
Questionsasked + if/whether (yes/no) or wh-word + statement order (no inversion, no "?")
Commandstold/ordered/requested + person + (not) to + V1

13.2 Active ↔ Passive voice

TensePassive formulaActive → Passive
Present simpleam/is/are + V3Lizards eat insects. → Insects are eaten by lizards.
Past simplewas/were + V3She wrote a letter. → A letter was written by her.
Present continuousam/is/are being + V3He is painting a wall. → A wall is being painted by him.
Past continuouswas/were being + V3He was playing the flute. → The flute was being played by him. 2025 Q7(2)
Present perfecthas/have been + V3They have built a dzong. → A dzong has been built by them.
Future simplewill be + V3They will announce the result. → The result will be announced.
Modal verbsmodal + be + V3You must finish it. → It must be finished.
{{compare:Fig 13.2 Active to passive: swap the object and the subject, change the verb|ACTIVE: Subject - Verb - Object|PASSIVE: Object - be + V3 - (by Subject)|He (subject);was playing (past continuous);the flute (object)|The flute (new subject);was being played (be + V3);by him (agent)|→}}
Other useful patternsStructure & example
No sooner… thanNo sooner had + subject + V3 + than + subject + V2 — No sooner had the bell rung than the students rushed out. (Same meaning: Hardly had… when…)
Although / though / even thoughAlthough + clause, main clause (never use but in the same sentence) — Although it rained, we played.
Despite / in spite of+ noun / V-ing: Despite the rain, we played.
Antonym rewriteReplace the adjective with its opposite and keep the meaning with not: predictable → not unpredictable.
Not only… but alsoNot only did she sing, but she also danced.
Unless= if… not: Unless you hurry, you will be late.

2025 Q5(vi)"Most lizards rely on camouflage to hide from their enemies." Which sentence is its passive form? A Camouflage is relied on by most lizards… B Camouflage was relied on… C Camouflage has been relied on… D Camouflage has been relied upon… [1]
Answer: A — the original is simple present, so the passive is is + V3 (is relied on). B is past, C and D are present perfect.

14.3 Grammar MCQ concepts

ConceptRuleExample
GerundV-ing used as a noun (subject/object)Painting landscapes allows me to express my creativity.
Modifieradjective/adverb that describes another wordThe furry puppy (furry modifies puppy)
Simple sentenceone independent clause (can have a compound subject/predicate)She enjoys painting and playing the guitar.
Compound sentencetwo independent clauses joined by and/but/soThe bus arrived late, so he missed the movie.
Complex sentenceindependent + dependent clauseAlthough it rained, we played.
Question tagpositive statement → negative tag; negative → positive; same auxiliary & subject as pronoun. No auxiliary → do/does/did.Good people speak well of others, don't they?
Double negativetwo negatives cancel each other — use one negative + anyHe didn't accept any suggestions. (didn't see nothing)
Pronoun agreement"each / everyone / every" is singular → his or her + singular nounEach of the students brought his or her own textbook.
Correlativesboth…and • either…or • neither…nor • not only…but also • whether…or notThis salad is both delicious and healthy.
whether / ifuse whether after prepositions and before to-infinitive or or nota decision about whether to go
Negative inversionNo longer / Never / Rarely / Hardly … + auxiliary + subjectNo longer do we expect…
