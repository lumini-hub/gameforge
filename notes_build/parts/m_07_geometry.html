Symmetry

  • Reflection (mirror) symmetry: a shape folds onto itself along a line of symmetry. Turn (rotational) symmetry: the shape fits onto itself when turned less than 360° about a centre; order of turn symmetry = number of times it fits in one full turn; angle of turn = 360° ÷ order.
  • Regular polygon with n sides: n lines of reflection and turn symmetry of order n. Regular hexagon: 6 mirror lines (3 through opposite vertices, 3 through midpoints of opposite sides), turn order 6 (60°), and it is also point symmetric (180°) [[P25 Q7a]].
  • A figure made of 8 identical leaves around a centre has 8 mirror lines [[P24 Q1ix: C]]. A hexagonal nut has turn symmetry of order 6 (A in P23 Q1xv, but the item mentions “three rotations” — (check): if the total number of fits in three turns is meant it is 6 × 3 = 18).
  • Both horizontal and vertical mirror lines: 4-point star, rhombus, cross, rectangle, bow-tie (not heart, pentagon, trapezium) [[P25 Q1xviii: B]].

  • The point where the three paths from midpoints to the opposite corners meet = centroid [[P24 Q1v: C]]. Circumcentre of a right-angled triangle = midpoint of the hypotenuse [[P25 Q1x: C]] [[P23 Q1xvi: A]].
  • Angle facts: angles of a triangle add to 180°; an exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles; angles on a line add to 180°.

Constructing a triangle and its circumcircle

  • Draw the base with a ruler, measure the given angles with a protractor (or construct with compasses), join to form the triangle. Draw the perpendicular bisectors of two sides (arcs above and below each side, join); where they meet is the circumcentre O. With radius OA draw the circle through A, B, C. Keep all arcs.
