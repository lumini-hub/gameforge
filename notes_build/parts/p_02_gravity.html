2.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Law of universal gravitationEvery body attracts every other body with a force that is directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres: F = G m1m2 / r². G = 6.67 × 10−11 N m² kg−2.
Two factors(1) the masses of the two bodies, (2) the distance between them. Double both masses → F becomes 4×. Double the distance → F becomes ¼.
Weight and gW = mg. g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (≈ 10) on Earth and about 1.6 m/s² on the Moon. g does not depend on the mass of the falling body.
Free fallMotion under the action of gravity alone (air resistance negligible, e.g. a coin on the Moon, a hammer and a feather in the Apollo 15 demonstration). All bodies fall with the same constant acceleration g; velocity increases uniformly: v = g t (from rest).
Air resistance (drag)Opposes the fall; increases with speed and surface area. A sheet of paper falls more slowly than a paper ball of the same mass because its larger area gives a larger drag.
Terminal velocityAs speed grows, drag grows until drag = weight (driving force). Net force = 0 → acceleration = 0 → the body falls with a constant velocity. Before it: net force and acceleration decrease but are not zero.
MicrogravityIn space an astronaut's pen cannot write because the ink is not pulled to the nib by gravity.
FormulaUse
F = G m1m2 / r²gravitational force; to find a mass: m2 = F r² / (G m1)
W = m g    v = g t    h = ½ g t²    v² = 2 g hfree fall (from rest)

2.2 Solved questions — Section A

2023 MCQ-iii || Object X falls through air of density 1.37 (P), 1.27 (Q), 1.36 (R), 1.75 (S) kg/m³ from the same height. In which case does it reach the ground first? || B (Q) || Air resistance is smallest in the least dense air (1.27 kg/m³), so the object accelerates most and arrives first. 2023 MCQ-xxiii || If the masses of two objects are doubled, the gravitational force… || B || F ∝ m1m2 → 2 × 2 = 4: it increases by a factor of four. 2025 MCQ-iii || "Free fall" is the condition in which… || B || the motion of a body is due to gravity alone, when air resistance is negligible. 2025 MCQ-xvi || When do falling objects attain terminal velocity? || B || When the driving force (weight) equals the drag force: net force zero, velocity constant. 2023 T/F-i || The acceleration of a falling body at terminal velocity is zero. || TRUE — weight and drag balance, net force is zero. 2024 Fill-ii || Acceleration due to gravity for a falling object is unaffected by its… || mass. 2024 T/F-iv || A falling coin on the Moon is an example of free fall. || TRUE — the Moon has no air, so only gravity acts. 2024 Match-ii || Acceleration due to gravity is zero (Column A) || c. terminal velocity — at terminal velocity the net force and hence the acceleration of the falling body is zero. 2025 Fill-ii || A body that falls at a constant velocity will be falling under the sole influence of… || gravity (expected answer). Note: strictly, a body at constant (terminal) velocity has balanced forces; "sole influence of gravity" describes free fall with constant acceleration.

2.3 Solved questions — Section B

2023 Q3(f) || Why can't astronauts write with a normal pen in space? || A normal pen needs gravity to make the ink flow to the nib. In space (microgravity) the ink does not flow down, so the pen does not write. 2023 Q5(a) || A cat falling from a building is not injured. Why? || (1) Its small mass and large body area give a low terminal velocity (air resistance soon balances its weight), so it does not fall faster and faster. (2) It spreads its body and lands on its flexible legs, bending them to increase the time of stopping and reduce the force of impact. 2023 Q7(b) || An object falls from the same height on the Moon and on the Earth. Compare the velocities and give a reason. || The object reaches the greater velocity on the Earth because g is larger on the Earth (about 10 m/s² against 1.6 m/s² on the Moon). Using v = √(2gh), a larger g gives a larger v. 2023 Q8(a) || Paper sheet vs paper ball dropped from 100 m: complete the table. || Mass: equal. Force exerted on the ground (weight): equal. Time taken to reach the ground: sheet takes more than the ball. Drag force: sheet experiences more than the ball (larger surface area). 2024 Q3(a) || TWO factors that influence the law of universal gravitation. || (1) The masses of the two bodies (F ∝ m1m2). (2) The distance between them (F ∝ 1/r²). 2024 Q4(a) || Rain droplets are evenly spaced near the ground but irregular near the cloud. Explain. || Near the cloud the drops are still accelerating (weight greater than drag), so they spread out irregularly. After falling some distance they reach terminal velocity (drag = weight, net force zero) and fall at a constant speed, so they are evenly spaced. 2024 Q6(e) || Does velocity remain constant during free fall? || No. In free fall the body has a constant acceleration g (about 10 m/s²), so its velocity increases by about 10 m/s every second (until air resistance produces terminal velocity). 2024 Q7(e) || F = 500 N, r = 10 × 106 m, MEarth = 6 × 1024 kg, G = 6.67 × 10−11. Find the mass of the satellite. || F = G M m / r² ⇒ m = F r² / (G M) = 500 × (107)² / (6.67 × 10−11 × 6 × 1024) = 5 × 1016 / 4.0 × 1014 = ≈ 125 kg. 2024 Q9(b) || Which graph shows a ball falling from a height to terminal velocity? || Graph B: the velocity rises quickly at first, the slope decreases as drag increases, and the graph becomes flat at a constant (terminal) velocity. Graph A would mean velocity keeps increasing (no drag); Graph C shows the velocity decreasing, which does not happen in a fall. 2025 Q4(a) || Fill in the speed table (0 s: 0; 1 s: 9.8; 2 s: ?; 3 s: ?; 4 s: 39.2) and conclude. || (i) 19.6 m/s (ii) 29.4 m/s. Conclusion: the speed of a falling body increases by 9.8 m/s every second, i.e. it has a constant acceleration g = 9.8 m/s² (v = g t). 2025 Q7(b) || TWO motion characteristics of a free-falling body. || (1) It moves with a constant acceleration g (about 9.8 m/s²) downwards. (2) The acceleration is independent of mass, so all bodies fall at the same rate (velocity increases uniformly with time). 2025 Q7(c) || Gravitational force between a 60 kg man and a 20 kg dog 0.50 m apart (G = 6.67 × 10−11). || F = G m1m2/r² = 6.67 × 10−11 × 60 × 20 / (0.50)² = 6.67 × 10−11 × 1200 / 0.25 = 3.2 × 10−7 N (very small). 2025 Q8(a) || A falling leaf: how do the net force and acceleration change before and after terminal velocity? || Before: weight (driving force) > drag, so the net force is downward and non-zero; it decreases as drag increases, so the acceleration is non-zero but decreasing. After reaching terminal velocity: drag = driving force, so the net force = 0 and acceleration = 0; the leaf falls at a constant velocity.
