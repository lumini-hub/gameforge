Meaning and components

Public finance = the income (revenue) and expenditure of the government at all levels (central, district, local). Its three components are budget, public revenue and public expenditure [[P24 Q1d(iv): TRUE]].

ExamplesPersonal Income Tax (PIT), Business Income Tax (BIT), Corporate Income Tax (CIT), land tax, cattle tax, property taxSales tax, excise duty, import and export (custom) duty, entertainment tax [[P24 Q1a(iii): D]]
  • Tax = compulsory payment to the government by people, firms and corporations for development and social welfare [[P25 Q1d(iv): TRUE]]. Corporate employee, wholesale owner and doctor all pay tax on income → direct tax [[P25 Q1b(iii): direct]].
  • Non-tax revenue: administrative fees and charges (birth registration, land transaction, vehicle registration, court fees); revenue from government departments (electricity, public transport, water); grants (voluntary, for a specific purpose); borrowing (internal and external) to fill the gap between planned expenditure and revenue.
  • Grants help infrastructure but cannot solve all challenges [[P25 Q1a(xii): C]]. Excessive borrowing raises interest payments [[P24 Q1a(xii): D]].
  • Benefits of paying tax: funds roads, free education and health; narrows the gap between rich and poor; reduces dependence on aid (Denmark: high tax, narrow wealth gap, high trust).

Public expenditure

Current expenditureCapital expenditure
regular running of government within a year: salaries of civil servants, interest payments, subsidies, medicines, defence, road maintenance [[P24 Q1b(iii): current]]creation of capital assets: roads, bridges, airports, school buildings, dzongs, equipment, hydropower plants

Aims: reduce regional disparities, build infrastructure, develop human resources, give social security, promote the private sector.

Budget

  • Budget = annual financial statement of estimated revenue and proposed expenditure for a year. Supplementary budget = extra budget for unforeseen situations (natural disasters, extra activities, inflation) — not for planned development activities [[P24 Q1d(v): FALSE]]. A high supplementary budget may show poor planning.
  • Importance: (i) proper planning and allocation of resources by priority; (ii) a framework for implementing, monitoring and evaluating programmes (sound financial management); (iii) shows the financial status and gives transparency and accountability.

Solved questions — short answers and Section B

Sort: Taxes: excise duty, corporate income tax; Non-taxes: borrowing, commercial revenue (1 mark for each correct pair). Will borrowing reduce regional disparities? Yes, if it is spent on the right things. (1) Loans used for roads, schools, hospitals and electricity in less developed areas improve services and growth there; (2) the projects create jobs and markets in lagging regions. (But borrowing must be repaid with interest and may increase debt, so it should not be spent on consumption.) Is raising taxes a practical way to stimulate the economy? Not really. (1) Higher taxes reduce disposable income, so households spend and save less, and demand falls; (2) they discourage investment and business as profits fall. A better way is more public spending, lower interest and widening the tax base. (It does raise revenue for the government.) Efficient use of resources at school: (1) save electricity and water (switch off fans and lights, close taps) and reduce paper (use both sides, reuse); (2) avoid food waste, reuse and recycle, look after furniture and books, and share stationery. [2] Importance of a budget: (1) it plans and allocates resources according to social and economic priorities [1]; (2) it is a framework to implement, monitor and evaluate programmes and gives transparency and accountability; it shows the financial status of the government [1]. Can a higher property tax reduce income inequality? Yes, I agree. (1) It is progressive: those who own more property (the rich) pay more, and the money is used for education, health and rural development that help the poor; (2) it discourages hoarding of land and speculation, so housing and land become easier to get. [2] Property tax by value (Property Tax Act 2023): beneficial. (1) More revenue for the government without burdening the poor, and a fairer sharing of tax (those who own more pay more); (2) it reduces income and wealth inequality and discourages keeping land idle. [2] Revenue Nu 52,626.582 m; expenditure Nu 69,156.613 m: a deficit of Nu 16,530.031 million (expenditure − revenue). Recommend increasing tax revenue (regular, domestic, no repayment). Measures: (1) widen the tax base — bring informal businesses and more earners into the tax net; (2) improve collection and stop evasion with online filing, audits and penalties; (3) review tax rates — e.g. a progressive property tax and higher tax on luxury goods. (Non-tax: raise fees and commercial income, e.g. electricity tariff, tourism fees.) [3 × 1]
