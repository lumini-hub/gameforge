Chapter 6: Current Electricity — Ohm's Law, Resistance & Heating EffectV–I graphs and a current / power calculation appear every year

6.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Electric current (I)The rate of flow of electric charge in a circuit: I = Q / t. Unit: ampere (A). Conventional current flows from the positive to the negative terminal; electrons flow from the negative (higher electron concentration) towards the positive terminal.
Potential difference (V)Energy given per unit charge; unit: volt (V).
Resistance (R)Opposition to current: R = V / I; unit: ohm (Ω). R depends on length (R ∝ L), cross-sectional area (R ∝ 1/A: a thicker wire has less resistance), material and temperature.
Ohm's lawAt constant temperature (and other physical conditions) the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference: V ∝ I, i.e. V / I = R is constant. The V–I graph is a straight line through the origin; the slope of V–I = R. Triangle: V = I × R.
Ohmic and non-ohmicOhmic: copper, nichrome (constant V/I). Non-ohmic: filament lamp, diode, thermistor, tungsten at high current (V/I changes) — "variable resistance".
Heating effectH = I² R t: depends on current, resistance and time. A filament lamp has a thin, high-resistance tungsten filament, so it becomes white/red hot.
Electrical powerP = V I = I² R = V² / R. Current drawn: I = P / V.
FormulaUse
V = I R    I = Q / t    P = V Icircuit calculations
H = I² R theat produced

Chapter 7: Electromagnetism, Generators & TransformersA transformer calculation and a Fleming-rule question appear every year

7.1 Must-know summary

TermMeaning / rule
Motor effectA current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field experiences a force. Direction by Fleming's left-hand rule: Thumb = Force (motion), First finger = Field (N→S), Second finger = Current. Reversing the current (or the poles) reverses the direction of the force/rotation; reversing both leaves it unchanged. Used in the electric motor.
Electromagnetic inductionAn emf (and current) is induced in a coil whenever the magnetic flux through it changes (magnet moved in or out of a solenoid, a conductor cutting field lines). Faraday's law. Direction by Fleming's right-hand rule: Thumb = Motion, First finger = Field, Second finger = Induced current.
GeneratorRotating a coil in a magnetic field induces an emf. AC generator: slip rings and carbon brushes → alternating current. DC generator: a split-ring commutator → direct current. The emf is greatest when the coil is parallel to the field (cutting lines fastest).
TransformerChanges AC voltage using two coils on an iron core. Vs/Vp = Ns/Np. Step-up: Ns > Np; step-down: Ns < Np (mobile charger). Ideal (100 %): VpIp = VsIs, so voltage up means current down. Works only with AC.
Why not 100 % efficientHeat in the coils (I²R, copper loss), eddy currents in the core, hysteresis loss, flux leakage, and sound/vibration.
Step-up for transmissionThe same power P = V I is sent at a very high voltage, so the current is small and the heat loss I²R in the long lines is small. A step-down transformer then lowers the voltage for homes.
FormulaUse
Vs/Vp = Ns/Npvoltage / turns
VpIp = VsIscurrents of an ideal transformer
