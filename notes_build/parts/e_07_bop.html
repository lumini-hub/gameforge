Balance of payments (BoP)

BoP = a systematic record of all economic transactions between the residents of a country and the rest of the world in a year: receipts for exports, services and capital received; payments for imports, services and capital sent out.

[[fig:e_bop|Components of the balance of payments.]]
  • Current account: value of goods and services, income and current transfers. Capital account: capital transfers and non-produced, non-financial assets. Financial account: direct investment, portfolio investment, reserve assets. A deficit in the current account must be met by a surplus on the capital account.
  • Surplus (favourable): receipts (credit) > payments (debit). Deficit (unfavourable): payments > receipts. In accounting terms the BoP always balances; in reality it is in surplus or deficit.
  • Consequences of a big deficit: demand for foreign exchange exceeds supply → depreciation of the domestic currency, fall in foreign exchange reserves, more borrowing and debt, higher prices of imports [[P25 Q1a(xv): D]].

Balance of tradeBalance of payments
Coversonly exports and imports of goods (visible) and servicesall transactions: trade, services, income, transfers, capital and financial flows
Part ofa component of the current accountthe whole record (current + capital + financial)
Resultfavourable if exports > imports; unfavourable if imports > exportssurplus or deficit

Net export = exports (X) − imports (M). Negative = trade deficit.

Exchange rate

  • Exchange rate = the rate at which one currency is exchanged for another (the price of a foreign currency in our currency), set by demand for and supply of currency; it changes daily.
  • Appreciation = the value of our currency rises against another (we get more foreign currency per Nu). Depreciation = value falls.
  • Depreciation: imports become expensive (goods and services cost more at home) and exports become cheap for foreigners [[P25 Q1a(vii): C]]. Appreciation: imports cheap, exports expensive.
  • Roles: needed for international payments; a sign of economic health; affects trade; a stable rate attracts foreign capital and helps diversification.
  • Conversion: foreign amount × rate = amount in Nu.

Importance of foreign exchange

Foreign currency earned by exports is needed to pay for imports (fuel, machines, medicines), repay foreign loans, keep reserves and a stable exchange rate, finance development and study abroad.
