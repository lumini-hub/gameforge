Chapter 1: Expository EssayQ1 · 25 marks · 350–400 words · asked in 2023, 2024 and 2025

1.1 Must-know summary

PointWhat to remember
What is an expository essay?An essay that explains, informs or discusses a topic using facts, reasons and examples. It does not tell a story and it does not simply argue "for/against".
Instruction words in the papersHow has…? (cause–effect) • Explain THREE benefits (exactly three) • The value of… (importance) • Discuss its impacts (effects) • The role of… • The importance of…
Marks are given for(1) orderly and coherent presentation of content, (2) essay features (introduction, body, conclusion, topic sentences, linking words), (3) accuracy of grammar and spelling.
Length350–400 words ≈ 5 paragraphs ≈ 3 pages of the answer booklet. Too short loses content marks; too long wastes time.
StructureIntroduction (60–80 words) → 3 body paragraphs (80–90 words each) → Conclusion (50–60 words).
Tense & personMostly simple present, third person (people, youth, Bhutan). Avoid "I", "you" and slang.
Do NOTMention your name, school, village, teacher or any detail that identifies you → the answer can be cancelled.

2023 Q1(a)How has social media become a major cause of insecurities among today's youth? [25]

Plan: Intro → (1) constant comparison → (2) unrealistic beauty standards & likes = self-worth → (3) cyberbullying & fear of missing out → Conclusion.

Social Media and the Rise of Youth Insecurity

Social media has changed the way young people communicate, learn and entertain themselves. Platforms such as Facebook, Instagram and TikTok connect teenagers with friends and celebrities within seconds. However, behind the smiling photographs and the number of likes lies a growing problem. Social media has become a major cause of insecurity among today's youth because it encourages endless comparison, promotes unrealistic standards of beauty, and exposes young people to cyberbullying and the fear of missing out.

Firstly, social media breeds insecurity through constant comparison. Users usually post only the best moments of their lives, such as holidays, new clothes and celebrations. When a teenager scrolls through these polished pictures, he or she begins to feel that his or her own life is dull and inadequate. For instance, a student who sees classmates posting photographs from expensive trips may feel poor or left behind. Over time, such comparisons weaken self-confidence and make young people dissatisfied with what they have.

Secondly, filters and edited photographs create unrealistic standards of beauty. Many youth believe that the flawless faces and perfect bodies they see online are real. As a result, they become unhappy with their own appearance, and some even starve themselves or depend on expensive cosmetic products to look "perfect". Likewise, the number of likes and followers has become a measure of self-worth. A teenager whose post receives only a few likes may wrongly conclude that he or she is unpopular or unattractive.

Thirdly, social media exposes the youth to cyberbullying and the fear of missing out. Hurtful comments, rumours and embarrassing photographs can spread rapidly and remain online for years, leaving victims anxious and ashamed. In addition, when young people see their friends attending parties without them, they fear being excluded. This fear compels them to stay online constantly, which disturbs their sleep, studies and real-life relationships.

In conclusion, although social media offers many benefits, its careless use has made many young people feel inadequate, lonely and anxious. Parents and teachers should guide the youth to use these platforms wisely, while the youth themselves must remember that online lives are rarely the full truth. By valuing real friendships and their own talents, they can enjoy technology without losing their confidence.

2023 Q1(b)Explain THREE benefits for Bhutan if her people chose to use electric vehicles over fossil-fuel vehicles. [25]

Plan: Intro → (1) saves foreign currency / uses our own hydropower → (2) protects environment (carbon-neutral pledge) → (3) health & quiet towns → Conclusion.

Why Bhutan Should Choose Electric Vehicles

Bhutan is proudly carbon negative, yet the number of petrol and diesel vehicles on its roads grows every year. Since all fuel has to be imported, switching to electric vehicles (EVs) would be one of the wisest decisions the nation could make. If the Bhutanese people chose electric vehicles over fossil-fuel vehicles, the country would benefit in three important ways: it would save foreign currency, protect the environment, and improve public health and well-being.

The first and most obvious benefit is economic. Bhutan has no oil of its own and spends a large amount of its hard-earned foreign currency, mainly Indian rupees, on importing petrol and diesel. Electric vehicles run on electricity, and Bhutan produces plenty of clean hydropower. By charging vehicles with domestic electricity, the country would reduce its fuel import bill and keep that money at home. Moreover, EV owners would spend less on running costs because electricity is cheaper than fuel and electric motors need less maintenance than engines.

The second benefit is environmental protection. Petrol and diesel vehicles release carbon dioxide and other harmful gases that pollute the air and contribute to global warming. Electric vehicles produce no exhaust emissions. Since Bhutan's electricity comes from rivers rather than coal, the vehicles would run on truly clean energy. This would help Bhutan to keep its promise to remain carbon neutral for all time and to protect its forests, glaciers and rivers, which are the foundation of its tourism and agriculture.

The third benefit relates to health and quality of life. Smoke and dust from vehicles in busy towns such as Thimphu and Phuentsholing cause coughing, asthma and other breathing problems, especially among children and the elderly. EVs would clean the air in these crowded places. They are also much quieter than conventional vehicles, so towns would have less noise pollution. A cleaner and calmer environment would help people to live healthier and more peaceful lives, and would make Bhutan even more attractive to visitors.

In conclusion, electric vehicles offer Bhutan a rare chance to save money, protect nature and improve the health of its citizens at the same time. The government can support this change through subsidies, charging stations and awareness programmes, but the real change must come from the people. By choosing electric vehicles, every Bhutanese can contribute to a greener and more self-reliant nation.

2024 Q1(a)The value of honouring our tradition in the modern world. [25]

Plan: Intro → (1) identity → (2) moral values & harmony → (3) economy (tourism, Zorig Chusum) → tradition + technology together → Conclusion.

The Value of Honouring Our Tradition in the Modern World

Bhutan is a small Himalayan kingdom, but its culture is rich and unique. Every day we see people wearing the gho and kira, greeting elders with respect and celebrating tshechus with joy. Today, however, television, the internet and fast-changing lifestyles are pulling the young generation towards foreign ways. In such a time, honouring our tradition is not outdated; it is essential because it gives us our identity, teaches us moral values, supports our economy and connects one generation to the next.

First, tradition gives a people their identity. A nation without its culture is like a tree without roots. Our national dress, language, architecture and festivals show the world who we are. When a young Bhutanese proudly wears the gho or kira to school and the dzong, he or she carries a sense of belonging and pride. In a globalised world where people are becoming alike, this identity helps us to stand apart and to remain confident about ourselves.

Second, traditions teach values that modern life often forgets. Customs such as driglam namzha, respect for parents and elders, and sharing food and labour with neighbours build harmony and kindness in society. Religious practices and festivals bring families and villagers together and remind us of compassion, gratitude and the law of cause and effect. These values guide the youth to become responsible citizens, which no machine or application can teach.

Third, tradition contributes to our economy. Thousands of tourists visit Bhutan every year to witness our tshechus, see our dzongs and buy handwoven textiles, thangkas and wood carvings from the thirteen traditional arts and crafts, the Zorig Chusum. These skills create jobs for artisans, farmers and guides, especially in rural areas. If we neglect our traditions, we will lose both our culture and a valuable source of income.

Finally, honouring tradition does not mean rejecting modernity. We can use technology to record folk songs, teach dances online and promote our crafts to the world. The wise path is to take the best from the modern world while keeping our roots firm. In conclusion, tradition is the foundation upon which a strong and happy nation stands, and it is the duty of every Bhutanese to protect it and pass it on to future generations.

2024 Q1(b)Adolescents are at risk from cyberbullying. Discuss its impacts. [25]

Plan: Intro (define cyberbullying) → (1) mental health → (2) studies → (3) body & social life, lasting online record → Conclusion with solutions.

The Impacts of Cyberbullying on Adolescents

Adolescence is a sensitive stage of life when young people are searching for their identity and acceptance. With smartphones and social media in almost every teenager's hand, this stage now has a new danger: cyberbullying. It means harassing, insulting or threatening someone through messages, posts, photographs or videos online. Unlike traditional bullying, it follows the victim home, can be anonymous and spreads quickly. Its impacts on adolescents are serious and affect their mind, body, studies and relationships.

The most serious impact is on mental health. Victims often feel ashamed, helpless and afraid because insulting messages can be seen by hundreds of people. They may develop anxiety, depression and low self-esteem, and may begin to believe the cruel things said about them. In extreme cases, the pain becomes so unbearable that some adolescents think of hurting themselves or even ending their lives.

Cyberbullying also damages academic performance. A student who is constantly worried about the next hurtful message cannot concentrate in class. Many victims lose interest in lessons, skip school to avoid classmates and see their marks fall. Teachers may mistake this sudden change for laziness, and the real cause remains hidden.

Moreover, it affects physical health and social life. Stress leads to headaches, stomach aches, loss of appetite and sleepless nights. Socially, victims withdraw from friends and family, stop taking part in sports or cultural activities and prefer to remain alone. Some even respond with anger and become bullies themselves, which continues the cycle. Another cruel feature is that humiliating posts may stay on the internet for years and harm a person's reputation in the future.

To protect adolescents, parents should talk openly with their children and monitor online activities with care. Schools must run awareness programmes and offer counselling, and victims should save evidence, block the bully and report to a trusted adult or the authorities. Young people must also think before they post, because a single careless comment can hurt a life. In conclusion, cyberbullying is a silent but dangerous threat, and only the combined efforts of families, schools and the youth themselves can make the internet a safe place for everyone.

2025 Q1(a)The role of young people in the economic, social and political growth of a country. [25]

Plan: Intro (youth = present and future) → (1) economic: workforce, innovation, start-ups → (2) social: volunteering, awareness, culture → (3) political: democracy, voting, future leaders → Conclusion.

The Role of Young People in a Country's Growth

Young people are often called the future of a nation, but in truth they are also its present. In Bhutan, a large part of the population is under thirty, and their energy, ideas and skills can shape the direction of the country. Young people play vital roles in the economic, social and political growth of a nation, and a country that guides and trusts its youth moves forward with confidence.

Economically, the youth are the main workforce and the source of innovation. They bring new ideas, learn technology quickly and start small businesses in areas such as farming, tourism, handicrafts and information technology. When a young person starts a cafe, a poultry farm or a mobile application, he or she not only earns a living but also creates jobs for others and reduces unemployment. Educated youth who return to farming or work in the hydropower and service sectors also help the nation to depend less on imports and foreign aid.

Socially, young people are the energy behind change. They volunteer in clean-up campaigns, blood donation drives, tree-plantation programmes and community service, as seen in the De-suung programme and school clubs of Bhutan. Through social media and school activities, they spread awareness about drugs, mental health, gender equality and the environment. They also carry our culture forward by learning traditional arts, speaking our languages and celebrating our festivals, so that our identity is never lost.

Politically, the youth help to strengthen democracy. Bhutan became a democracy in 2008, and its success depends on citizens who are informed and responsible. Young voters can question leaders, vote carefully and take part in debates and youth forums. Many will one day become leaders, civil servants and lawmakers themselves, so the habits of honesty, dialogue and respect for the law that they develop now will decide the quality of tomorrow's governance.

However, young people can succeed only when they receive quality education, skills training and opportunities. Elders and the government must listen to them, while the youth must remain disciplined and committed. In conclusion, the economic prosperity, social harmony and political stability of a country largely depend on its youth, and by investing in them a nation invests in its own future.

2025 Q1(b)The importance of resilience, teamwork and communication for succeeding in life. [25]

Plan: Intro (three legs of a stool) → (1) resilience → (2) teamwork → (3) communication → how the three connect → Conclusion.

Resilience, Teamwork and Communication: Keys to a Successful Life

Success in life rarely comes from talent alone. A student may be intelligent, yet fail to progress if he or she gives up after the first failure, cannot work with others or cannot express ideas clearly. Three qualities, namely resilience, teamwork and communication, work together like the three legs of a stool to support a successful life. Each of them is explained below.

Resilience is the ability to recover from setbacks and keep trying. Life is full of disappointments, such as a low score in an examination, a lost match or a rejected application. Resilient people treat these as lessons rather than the end of the road. For example, a student who fails a test and studies harder the next time will eventually improve. Great achievers, such as sportspeople and scientists, often failed many times before succeeding. Resilience also protects mental health because it helps us to handle stress and disappointment calmly.

Teamwork is the ability to work with others towards a common goal. Most big achievements, from building a dzong to winning a football tournament, need many people with different skills. In a team, members share work, support one another and combine ideas so that the result is better than what one person could achieve alone. Teamwork also teaches us patience, responsibility and respect for different opinions, which are needed at school, at the workplace and at home.

Communication means expressing our thoughts clearly and listening carefully to others. Good communication avoids misunderstandings, builds trust and helps us to solve problems peacefully. A student who can speak confidently in an interview, write a clear letter and listen to a teacher's advice has a great advantage. In a team, communication is what holds members together, because plans cannot be carried out if nobody knows what others are doing.

These three qualities are closely connected. Communication allows a team to share its plans, teamwork gives each member support, and resilience helps the group to rise again after failures. In conclusion, anyone who wishes to succeed in education, work and life should develop all three, for knowledge alone cannot carry a person very far.
