Radicals (surds): rules

  • Product / quotient: √a × √b = √ab; √a ÷ √b = √ab. A radical is simplified when no square (or cube) factor is left under the root: √50 = 5√2; ∛40 = 2∛5.
  • Like surds add and subtract: 8√y-type terms are first simplified, then the like surds are combined.
  • Entire radical: put the number under the root: a√b = √a²b (3√5 = √45).
  • Powers of a root: √xn = xn/2; √(2m) is a whole number when m is even and contains a radical when m is odd.
  • Perfect cube: every prime exponent is a multiple of 3.

2 · Linear Equations, Systems & Functions
P25 Q1iv,ix,Q2d,Q3c,Q6a,Q7c · P24 Q1iv,xiv,Q4c,Q6a,Q7b,Q9d · P23 Q1iv,vi,vii,Q2b,Q3b,Q6b

Key ideas

  • Function: a rule giving exactly one output for each input. Vertical-line test: a graph is a function if no vertical line meets it twice. In a mapping diagram every input must have one and only one arrow.
  • Linear model: y = mx + c: m = slope = riserun, c = y-intercept (starting value). Cost models: total = fixed charge + rate × quantity.
  • Slope-intercept form: solve for y. 5x + 2y = 12 → y = −52x + 6. Intercepts: x = 0 gives the y-intercept; y = 0 gives the x-intercept.
  • Systems of equations: substitution (make one variable the subject) or elimination (equalise coefficients and add/subtract). The solution is the point of intersection of the two lines.
  • Word problems: define variables → write equations → solve → check in the problem.

Three forms of a quadratic

FormShows
Standard f(x) = ax² + bx + cy-intercept = c; a > 0 opens up, a < 0 opens down
Factored f(x) = a(x − p)(x − q)roots / x-intercepts p and q; axis of symmetry x = p + q2
Vertex f(x) = a(x − h)² + vvertex (h, v); axis x = h
  • Roots: factorise or use x = −b ± √b² − 4ac2a. A quadratic with roots p and q is (x − p)(x − q) = 0.
  • Sketching: find roots, y-intercept (set x = 0), vertex (x halfway between roots), then draw a smooth parabola. Equivalent functions have the same expanded form.

Transformations of y = x² (mapping notation)

f(x) = a(x − h)² + vMapping (x, y) →
a: vertical stretch/compression; a < 0 reflection in the x-axis(x, ay)
h: horizontal shift (right if h > 0)(x + h, y)
v: vertical shift (up if v > 0)(x, y + v)
all together(x + h, ay + v)

4 · Matrices & Networks
P25 Q1i,vi,Q4c,Q9c · P24 Q1i,xv,Q3b · P23 Q1i,Q4b,Q9a

Key ideas

  • Order of a matrix = rows × columns. Addition / subtraction need the same order P24 Q1i: A; add or subtract matching entries. Scalar multiplication multiplies every entry; the order does not change, so (½A − 2B) for two 3 × p matrices has order 3 × p P24 Q1xv: C.
  • Identity matrix I: 1s on the main diagonal, 0 elsewhere. For 2 × 2: [1 0; 0 1] P25 Q1i: B. AI = IA = A.
  • Multiplication AB needs (columns of A) = (rows of B); the product has order (rows of A) × (columns of B). Each entry = row × column. In general AB ≠ BA.
  • Applications: cost = (quantities row) × (price matrix); points = (results table) × (points column).

Networks and adjacency matrices

  • Adjacency matrix: the entry in row X, column Y = the number of direct edges (routes) from X to Y. For an undirected link the entry is 1 in both directions.
  • One stop-over trips (two edges: X → Z → Y) = entries of the matrix M² = M × M.
  • To match a matrix with a network, read row by row: row A gives the edges that leave A.

Symmetry

  • Reflection (mirror) symmetry: a shape folds onto itself along a line of symmetry. Turn (rotational) symmetry: the shape fits onto itself when turned less than 360° about a centre; order of turn symmetry = number of times it fits in one full turn; angle of turn = 360° ÷ order.
  • Regular polygon with n sides: n lines of reflection and turn symmetry of order n. Regular hexagon: 6 mirror lines (3 through opposite vertices, 3 through midpoints of opposite sides), turn order 6 (60°), and it is also point symmetric (180°) P25 Q7a.
  • A figure made of 8 identical leaves around a centre has 8 mirror lines P24 Q1ix: C. A hexagonal nut has turn symmetry of order 6 (A in P23 Q1xv, but the item mentions “three rotations” — (check): if the total number of fits in three turns is meant it is 6 × 3 = 18).
  • Both horizontal and vertical mirror lines: 4-point star, rhombus, cross, rectangle, bow-tie (not heart, pentagon, trapezium) P25 Q1xviii: B.

8 · Data Management & Probability
P25 Q1xiii,xvi,xx,Q2c,Q3a,Q4a,Q5a,Q8b,Q9b · P24 Q1vi,xvi,Q3c,Q5a,b,Q7a,Q9b · P23 Q1xvii–xx,Q3a,Q5c,Q6c,Q7c,Q8d,Q9d

Statistics: key ideas

  • Five-number summary: minimum, Q1, median (Q2), Q3, maximum. For ungrouped data: sort, find the median; Q1 = median of the lower half, Q3 = median of the upper half (leave out the median when n is odd). Range = max − min; IQR = Q3 − Q1.
  • Grouped data: Median = L + if(n2 − c) — L lower boundary of the median class, i class width, f its frequency, c cumulative frequency before it. Q1 uses n4, Q3 uses 3n4. Mean = ΣfxΣf.
  • Box plot: box from Q1 to Q3, line at the median, whiskers to min and max. Equal whiskers and a central median → symmetric. Right-skewed (long tail to the right): median closer to Q1 and the right whisker longer P25 Q1xx: D. A smaller spread (range, IQR) means more consistent / reliable data.
  • Outliers change the mean a lot but the median hardly at all.
  • Scatter plot & correlation: points rising → positive; falling → negative; no pattern → none; the closer to a line the stronger (r near ±1). The line of best fit passes through the middle of the points (about equal points above and below) and is used for predictions. Independent variable on the x-axis.

Probability: key ideas

  • P(event) = favourable outcomestotal outcomes. Independent events: one does not change the other (with replacement; separate rolls): P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Dependent events (without replacement): P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B | A). Conditional: P(B | A) = P(A and B)P(A).
  • A dependent event changes the sample space and so changes the probability of the next event P25 Q3a.
  • Venn diagram: for P(M given S) use only the S circle as the new total.
